@29000
Nukui Fusasada ?-1555

@29001
A Hatakeyama vassal. Guarded his lord Yoshitsugu in the house's strife. Overthrew the Yusa, who plotted against the Nukui, and became the strongest in the house. Killed by his lord Yoshitsuna, who sought to regain power.

@29002
Nukui Kagetaka ?-1582

@29003
A Hatakeyama vassal. In the chaos of the senior vassals' power struggles he left and returned again and again. After Honnoji he entered Noto to win back his lands, but was killed by Sakuma Morimasa.

@29004
Miyoshi Kazumori 1547-1615

@29005
A Hatakeyama vassal of Noto; lord of Ikeda Castle in Etchu. Later served Uesugi Kenshin, then Sassa Narimasa, following Narimasa to Higo. After Narimasa's death he went home to Noto and lived in retirement.

@29006
Shimozuma Rairyu ?-?

@29007
A temple official of the Ishiyama Honganji. When Honganji Kennyo became abbot, he was chosen as a temple official with Raiju and Rairyo. Versed in the tea ceremony; held tea gatherings with Tsuda Sotatsu and others.

@29008
Honganji Shokei 1494-1574

@29009
Abbot who led the Ikko sect at Ganshoji in Nagashima, Ise. Fought Oda Nobunaga's attacks on Nagashima fiercely. Seeing no hope of victory, he is said to have left his followers and thrown himself into the Kiso River.

@29010
Hattori Tomosada ?-?

@29011
A Nagashima local lord of Ise. Gathered local samurai and fugitive warriors into the "Hattori Band" and, allied with the Ikko, resisted Oda Nobunaga. Many generals beaten by Nobunaga, such as Saito Tatsuoki, joined him.

@29012
Shimozuma Nakataka 1551-1616

@29013
Said to be Raisho's son. A temple official of the Honganji who distinguished himself in the Ishiyama War. Sealed the peace with Oda Nobunaga in blood for the abbot Kennyo. A gifted Noh player; wrote "Dobusho".

@29014
Shichiri Yoshichika ?-?

@29015
A temple official who led the Ikko of Kaga. When Tomita Nagashige of Fuchu rebelled against Oda Nobunaga and was isolated, he destroyed him and spread his power. Later beaten by Nobunaga's army against the Echizen Ikko.

@29016
Suzuki Shigeyasu ?-1580

@29017
A retainer of the Ishiyama Honganji, sent to Kaga to lead the Ikko uprising. Built Torigoe Castle as a military base and resisted Oda Nobunaga and others. Later killed by Shibata Katsuie.

@29018
Asakura Kagetoshi ?-1572

@29019
An Asakura vassal; Takakage's brother, adopted by Norikage (Soteki). As lord of Tsuruga Castle he fought with his adoptive father in Kaga and the capital region. Later took over Norikage's post as governor of Tsuruga.

@29020
Asakura Kagetaka ?-?

@29021
Of the Asakura kin. Took over from Asakura Soteki, who went home sick, as commander-in-chief against the Ikko. Fought hard to put down the uprising until peace was made through the shogunate's mediation.

@29022
Tomita Nagashige 1552-1575

@29023
An Asakura vassal. After the Asakura fell he followed Oda Nobunaga and won merit against the Nagashima Ikko. Unhappy with his treatment by the Oda, he rebelled, but was killed by the Ikko of Kaga.

@29024
Maeba Yoshitsugu ?-1574

@29025
An Asakura vassal. Among the first to submit to Oda Nobunaga's invasion of Echizen, he served as guide; for this he became Deputy Constable of Echizen. Attacked by the resentful Tomita Nagashige, he died in battle.

@29026
Yamazaki Naganori 1552-1620

@29027
Each time his lord's house fell he moved on, serving the Asakura, Akechi, Shibata, Maeda and Tokugawa. Took part in Honnoji and later in Shizugatake, Sekigahara and other battles.

@29028
Toda Shigemasa 1554-1625

@29029
A Maeda vassal; a master of Chujo-ryu swordsmanship known as "Master Echigo". First spear at the battle of Suemori Castle in Noto. Distinguished himself at Odawara, Sekigahara and Osaka too.

@29030
Takeda Nobutoyo 1514-?

@29031
Motomitsu's second son. Became Constable of Wakasa after crushing a revolt over the succession. Later fell out and fought with his son Yoshizumi, but peace was made and he came home.

@29032
Takeda Yoshizumi 1526-1567

@29033
Head of the Wakasa Takeda; married a sister of the Shogun Ashikaga Yoshiteru. Inherited after a struggle with his father Nobutoyo, ended by peace. Constant strife kept him from escorting Ashikaga Yoshiaki to Kyoto.

@29034
Takeda Motoaki 1552-1582

@29035
Yoshizumi's eldest son; succeeded on his father's death. Submitted when Oda Nobunaga invaded Wakasa and Echizen. After Honnoji he joined Akechi Mitsuhide, was defeated, captured and took his own life.

@29036
Awaya Katsuhisa ?-1585

@29037
A vassal of the Wakasa Takeda; lord of Kuniyoshi Castle. Later won over by Oda Nobunaga, he fought in many places with merit. After Honnoji he served Hideyoshi as a mounted guard.

@29038
Atsuji Sadayuki ?-1582

@29039
An Azai vassal; lord of Yamamotoyama Castle in Omi. Betrayed his lord during Oda Nobunaga's attack on Odani and went over to the Oda. After Honnoji he sided with Akechi Mitsuhide; killed with his son.

@29040
Miyabe Keijun 1528-1599

@29041
A monk of Mt. Hiei who returned to lay life and served Azai Nagamasa; later submitted to Oda Nobunaga. Led the vanguard of Hashiba Hideyoshi's western campaign and became lord of Tottori Castle.

@29042
Miyabe Nagafusa 1581-1634

@29043
A Toyotomi vassal; lord of Tottori Castle in Inaba, Keijun's son. Inherited when his father retired. At Sekigahara he went over to the West and attacked Fushimi Castle and others; stripped of his domain after the war.

@29044
Tanaka Yoshimasa 1548-1609

@29045
A Toyotomi vassal; on Ishida Mitsunari's recommendation, elder to Hidetsugu, then Hideyoshi's direct vassal. At Sekigahara he fought for the East and captured Mitsunari; given Yanagawa in Chikugo, 320,000 koku.

@29046
Terasawa Hirotaka 1563-1633

@29047
A Toyotomi vassal; built Nagoya Castle in Hizen for the Korean campaign. After Hideyoshi's death he drew close to Tokugawa Ieyasu and joined the East at Sekigahara. Later held Karatsu in Hizen, 120,000 koku.

@29048
Rokkaku Sadayori 1495-1552

@29049
Succeeded his brother Ujitsuna as head. Entered Kyoto with the Ashikaga Shogun, overpowered the Azai and Kyogoku and built his power in Omi. Also famed for ordering his branch castles by "breaking castles".

@29050
Rokkaku Yoshiharu 1545-1612

@29051
Yoshikata's heir. Lost his vassals' trust after murdering his chief elder Goto Katatoyo. Beaten by Oda Nobunaga, he fled to Kai. After the Takeda fell he became a storyteller to Toyotomi Hideyoshi.

@29052
Goto Takaharu ?-1589

@29053
A Rokkaku vassal; Katatoyo's second son. Succeeded after his father died in the "Kannonji Incident". With the Shindo, one of the "Two Fujis" and a senior vassal of Yoshiharu; submitted to Oda Nobunaga's march on Kyoto.

@29054
Shindo Sadaharu ?-1551

@29055
A Rokkaku vassal; lord of Konohama Castle in Omi, a senior vassal equal to Goto Katatoyo. Served since the time of Sadayori's father Takayori. Handled diplomacy; mediated between Enryakuji and the Hokke sect.

@29056
Gamo Sadahide 1508-1579

@29057
A Rokkaku vassal. Led Sadayori's vanguard in many places and won fame for bravery. When his lord Yoshiharu murdered Goto Katatoyo and unrest broke out, he mediated between Yoshiharu and the vassals.

@29058
Mikumo Shigemochi 1540-1603

@29059
A Rokkaku vassal; succeeded his brother Katamochi. Signed the "Rokkaku Code" with his father Sadamochi. After the Rokkaku fell he lived as a ronin, then served Oda Nobukatsu and Gamo Ujisato.

@29060
Ogawa Suketada ?-1601

@29061
Lord of Fuchu Castle in Iyo. Sided with Akechi Mitsuhide at Yamazaki, later became elder to Shibata Katsutoyo, then served Toyotomi Hideyoshi with credit at Komaki and elsewhere. Went over to the East at Sekigahara.

@29062
Yamazaki Kataie 1547-1591

@29063
A Rokkaku vassal; lord of Yamazaki Castle in Omi, later served Oda Nobunaga. Briefly submitted to Akechi Mitsuhide after Honnoji, but won merit under Hashiba Hideyoshi and became lord of Sanda Castle in Settsu.

@29064
Yamazaki Iemori 1567-1614

@29065
Lord of Sanda Castle in Settsu; Kataie's heir. Served Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Sided with the West at Sekigahara, but his in-law Ikeda Terumasa saved him from punishment; became lord of Wakasa domain in Inaba.

@29066
Takada Matabee 1589-1671

@29067
Founder of the Takada school of spearmanship; learnt the spear from Hozoin Inei. Made a ronin for joining the Toyotomi at Osaka, he opened a school in Edo; later served Ogasawara Tadamasa, lord of Kokura in Buzen.

@29068
Ashikaga Yoshiharu 1511-1550

@29069
12th Muromachi Shogun. Defeated by Miyoshi Motonaga, who rose in Awa, he fled to Omi. After Motonaga's death Hosokawa Harumoto brought him back to Kyoto, but he fell out with Harumoto too and resigned as Shogun.

@29070
Kyogoku Takatomo 1572-1622

@29071
Takayoshi's second son. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi with his brother Takatsugu; held Iida in Shinano, 100,000 koku. Fought for the East at Sekigahara; afterwards urged his brother, fled to Mt. Koya, to serve the Tokugawa.

@29072
Matsui Yasuyuki 1550-1612

@29073
An Ashikaga vassal versed in the tea ceremony. After Yoshiteru's violent death he served Hosokawa Fujitaka, distinguishing himself in the conquest of Tango. Fought for the East at Sekigahara; held Kitsuki in Bungo, 26,000 koku.

@29074
Nankobo Tenkai 1536-1643

@29075
A Tendai monk. Mastered the doctrine on Mt. Hiei and became a close adviser of Tokugawa Ieyasu. Worked behind the scenes for the Tokugawa: "the Black-Robed Chancellor". Some say he was Akechi Mitsuhide.

@29076
Hatano Harumichi ?-?

@29077
Lord of Yakami Castle in Tanba. Sided with Hosokawa Harumoto against Miyoshi Chokei. Repelled him many times, but at last submitted to Matsunaga Hisahide and was driven from his castle.

@29078
Hatano Hidenao ?-1579

@29079
Harumichi's second son. When Akechi Mitsuhide, under Oda Nobunaga, took Yakami Castle in Tanba, he surrendered with his brother Hideharu. Blamed by Nobunaga for going over to the Mori, he was executed at Azuchi.

@29080
Hatano Munetaka ?-1573

@29081
Of the Hatano kin. Built Hikami Castle in Tanba and became its lord. A brave general called "the Demon of Tanba"; said to have guarded the capital at the enthronement of Emperor Ogimachi.

@29082
Hosokawa Harumoto 1514-1563

@29083
Head of the Hosokawa Kanrei house; inherited after his father Sumimoto's death. Allied with Miyoshi Motonaga, set up the Shogun at Sakai and took power. Later fell out with the Miyoshi and lost to Chokei.

@29084
Hosokawa Akimoto 1548-1592

@29085
Head of the Hosokawa Kanrei house; Harumoto's heir. When Oda Nobunaga entered Kyoto with Ashikaga Yoshiaki, he joined him and married Nobunaga's sister Inu. Said to have been Nobunaga's kemari partner.

@29086
Kozai Motonari ?-1560

@29087
A vassal of the Hosokawa Kanrei. When Miyoshi Chokei drove Harumoto from Kyoto, he fought hard to regain power. Did well, beating Matsunaga Hisahide during his Tanba campaign, but was defeated and died in battle.

@29088
Kozai Yoshikiyo ?-1588

@29089
Motonari's grandson. Inherited at 8 as the 18th head. Blinded by illness, he was called "the Blind General". From his base at Shoga Castle in Sanuki he fought the Chosokabe.

@29090
Tsuda Shigehisa 1549-1634

@29091
Served the Hosokawa, the Miyoshi and the Ashikaga Shoguns before becoming an Akechi vassal. After Yamazaki he served Hashiba Hideyoshi and fought at Shizugatake. In old age a storyteller to the Maeda.

@29092
Ikeda Nagamasa ?-1563

@29093
Lord of Ikeda Castle in Settsu. Inherited after Hosokawa Harumoto forced his father Nobumasa to kill himself. For a time he dominated western Settsu, but had to submit to Miyoshi Chokei.

@29094
Ikeda Katsumasa ?-1578

@29095
Nagamasa's eldest son. Resisted Oda Nobunaga to the end but surrendered and kept his lands. Then served Nobunaga; with Itami Chikaoki and Wada Koremasa he was called one of the "Three Constables of Settsu".

@29096
Ikeda Tomomasa ?-1603

@29097
Nagamasa's second son. Served Oda Nobunaga, but when strife drove out his brother Katsumasa he became head, allied with the Miyoshi Triumvirate and turned on Nobunaga. Later followed Araki Murashige.

@29098
Nakagawa Kiyohide 1542-1583

@29099
An Oda vassal. Served Araki Murashige, but left him when Murashige rebelled and served Hashiba Hideyoshi. Distinguished himself at Yamazaki, but at Shizugatake his rashness got him killed.
