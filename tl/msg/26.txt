@26000
Itami Yasunao 1523-1596

@26001
Son of Masaoki, lord of Itami Castle, Settsu. Fled to Suruga as a boy to serve the Imagawa; navy magistrate under Ujizane. After the Imagawa fell, fleet admiral for the Takeda, then navy magistrate for the Tokugawa.

@26002
Akechi Hidemitsu ?-1582

@26003
Oda vassal. Joined his father-in-law Mitsuhide in the Honnoji Incident. Held Azuchi Castle during Yamazaki; on news of the defeat fell back to Sakamoto Castle, Omi. Handed its treasures to the besiegers and killed himself.

@26004
Udono Nagateru ?-1562

@26005
Imagawa vassal. After Okehazama the Mikawa warriors left the Imagawa one by one, but Nagateru stayed loyal to the end. Fought the Tokugawa army seeking to pacify Mikawa, and fell in defeat.

@26006
Akao Kiyotsuna ?-1573

@26007
Azai vassal; with Kaiho Tsunachika and Amenomori Yahee, one of the clan's "Three Generals". Built the Akao enclosure at Odani Castle, Omi. Captured when the Azai fell and beheaded.

@26008
Mashita Nagamori 1545-1615

@26009
Toyotomi vassal. As one of the Five Magistrates, he handled land surveys and more; in the Korean campaign he managed administration and military affairs. Sided with the West at Sekigahara; banished to Mt. Koya.

@26010
Udono Ujinaga 1549-1624

@26011
Son of Nagateru. Captured by the Tokugawa army when his father fell; he was exchanged for Ieyasu's wife Lady Tsukiyama and heir Nobuyasu and returned to Sunpu Castle. Later surrendered to Ieyasu; fought at Nagashino.

@26012
Wakizaka Yasuharu 1554-1626

@26013
Toyotomi vassal; one of the Seven Spears of Shizugatake. In Hideyoshi's Chugoku campaign he slew Akai Naomasa, "the Red Demon". Went over to the East at Sekigahara and kept his lands.

@26014
Natsuka Masaie ?-1600

@26015
Niwa vassal. After the Niwa declined he served the Toyotomi and became one of the Five Magistrates. A matchless reckoner, he ran land surveys. Sided with the West at Sekigahara; killed himself after the retreat.

@26016
Okabe Masatsuna 1542-1583

@26017
Imagawa vassal. Surrendered to the Takeda invading Suruga, then served Shingen at Mikatagahara and elsewhere. After the Takeda fell he served Tokugawa Ieyasu and helped pacify Kai.

@26018
Okabe Nagamori 1568-1632

@26019
Tokugawa vassal; son of Masatsuna. Won merit at Nagakute and elsewhere; when Ieyasu moved to Kanto he was given 12,000 koku in Kazusa and Shimosa. Later became lord of Kameyama Castle, Tanba.

@26020
Okudaira Sadayoshi 1537-1598

@26021
Father of Nobumasa. First an Imagawa vassal; later refused Takeda Katsuyori's overtures and served Tokugawa Ieyasu. His service was rewarded with Bushu Castle, Mikawa.

@26022
Katsurayama Ujimoto ?-?

@26023
Imagawa vassal; lord of Katsurayama Castle, Suruga. Set up a barrier at Fuji to control merchants and goods, protected shrines and temples, and more. Held east Suruga and built a position of his own.

@26024
Gamo Katahide 1534-1584

@26025
Son of Sadahide; a senior Rokkaku vassal named in the Rokkaku house code. Resisted Oda Nobunaga but lost; made peace with the Oda by sending his heir Ujisato as hostage, and kept his lands.

@26026
Gamo Hideyuki 1583-1612

@26027
Heir of Ujisato; Toyotomi vassal. For strife within his clan he was cut from Aizu, Mutsu (920,000 koku) to Utsunomiya, Shimotsuke (180,000). After Sekigahara he was returned to Aizu with 600,000.

@26028
Kyogoku Takayoshi 1508-1581

@26029
Ashikaga vassal; served Yoshiteru as an attendant. After Yoshiteru's violent death, he worked hard to set up Yoshiaki. After Yoshiaki became shogun, he clashed with Oda Nobunaga and retired.

@26030
Matsudaira Hirotada 1526-1549

@26031
Father of Tokugawa Ieyasu. Driven from Okazaki Castle by Nobusada, he returned under Imagawa Yoshimoto's protection. Fought Oda Nobuhide again and again until killed by a retainer.

@26032
Momii Norinari ?-1576

@26033
Hatano vassal; lord of Momii Castle, Tanba. Matched with Akai Naomasa, "the Red Demon", as "the Blue Demon". Fell when attacked during Hashiba Hideyoshi's invasion of Chugoku.

@26034
Miyoshi Yoshioki 1542-1563

@26035
Heir of Chokei; served as a shogunal companion. Won battles against the Rokkaku and Hatakeyama, but died suddenly at 22. Some say Matsunaga Hisahide poisoned him.

@26036
Aoyama Tadanari 1551-1613

@26037
Tokugawa vassal; father of Tadatoshi. Became head of his house at 21. Appointed Kanto magistrate with Honda Masanobu and others; helped lay the shogunate's financial base.

@26038
Ishikawa Ienari 1535-1609

@26039
Tokugawa vassal; uncle of Kazumasa. Won much merit, as in crushing the Mikawa Ikko uprising. With Ieyasu since his hostage days at Sunpu, he was called a retainer of matchless loyalty.

@26040
Ishikawa Yasunaga ?-1642

@26041
Eldest son of Kazumasa; served Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Inherited Matsumoto, Shinano (60,000 koku) on his father's death. Sided with the East at Sekigahara. Later attainted and exiled for concealing lands.

@26042
Hatakeyama Akitaka ?-1573

@26043
Second son of Masakuni. When his brother Takamasa was driven out by Yusa Nobunori and others, they made him lord of Takaya Castle, Kawachi. Later plotted to kill Nobunori but was murdered instead.

@26044
Susukida Kanesuke ?-1615

@26045
Toyotomi vassal. Failed to hold Bakurogafuchi Fort in the winter campaign at Osaka and was mocked as "the ornamental warrior". Fell fighting bravely in the summer campaign. Said to be the baboon-slayer Iwami Jutaro.

@26046
Ono Harunaga ?-1615

@26047
Toyotomi vassal. Led the Osaka side after Katagiri Katsumoto left Osaka Castle, and was a leader of the Toyotomi in the Osaka campaigns. When Osaka Castle fell, he died with his lord Hideyori.

@26048
Itakura Katsushige 1545-1624

@26049
Tokugawa vassal. A monk until his thirties, he became head of his house when his father and brother fell in battle. After serving as town magistrate of Sunpu and Edo, he became Kyoto deputy, watching the western lords.

@26050
Tsutsui Sadatsugu 1562-1615

@26051
Adopted son of Junkei, whom he succeeded. For his misconduct he was moved to Ueno in Iga, and later attainted. Secretly sided with Osaka in the winter campaign and was made to kill himself.

@26052
Ina Tadatsugu 1550-1610

@26053
Tokugawa vassal. An attendant of Ieyasu, he took part in civil rule; his land surveys, fief allotments and flood control in Kanto laid the shogunate's economic base. His methods were called the "Ina school".

@26054
Suzuki Shigetomo ?-?

@26055
Perhaps Sadayu's son. Served in Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Korean campaign. Sided with the West at Sekigahara and slew Torii Mototada, defender of Fushimi Castle. Later a ronin, then a Mito Tokugawa vassal.

@26056
Horinouchi Ujiyoshi 1549-1615

@26057
Lord of Shingu Castle, Kii; steward of Kumano Shingu shrine. Submitted to Toyotomi Hideyoshi and led a navy in Korea. Sided with the West at Sekigahara; attainted and placed in Kato Kiyomasa's custody.

@26058
Sakai Tadakatsu 1587-1662

@26059
Tokugawa vassal; first battle at Sekigahara. Served four shoguns from Ieyasu on; as senior councillor and great elder he shaped domestic and foreign affairs. The 4th shogun Ietsuna called him "the foremost of ministers".

@26060
Naito Kiyonari 1555-1608

@26061
Tokugawa vassal; page to Ieyasu. Trusted by Ieyasu, he became Hidetada's tutor and Kanto magistrate. Later fell under suspicion, lived in seclusion and died.

@26062
Makino Yasunari 1555-1609

@26063
Tokugawa vassal; lord of Ushikubo Castle, Mikawa. Joined Hidetada's army in the Sekigahara war and attacked Ueda Castle. Fought hard but broke orders; confined, later pardoned.

@26064
Yamaguchi Shigemasa 1564-1635

@26065
Lord of Hoshizaki Castle, Owari; adopted son of Shigekatsu. First a vassal of Sakuma Masakatsu, then of Oda Nobukatsu, then the Tokugawa. Fought under Hidetada at Sekigahara; later angered Ieyasu and was confined.

@26066
Amago Masahisa ?-1554

@26067
Eldest son of Kunihisa. Formed the Shingu band with his father, but too proud of his prowess, he clashed with his cousin Haruhisa. Fell when Haruhisa, deceived by Mori Motonari's plot, attacked his residence.

@26068
Shiba Yoshikane 1540-1600

@26069
Heir of Yoshimune. When Oda Nobutomo killed his father, he turned to Oda Nobunaga and fought Nobutomo. Later turned on Nobunaga, was banished and wandered the land, becoming a Christian on his travels.

@26070
Mori Hideyori 1541-1593

@26071
Said to be a son of Shiba Yoshimune. Served Oda Nobunaga and was given Ina in Shinano after the Takeda fell. After Honnoji he followed Toyotomi Hideyoshi on many campaigns and became lord of Iida Castle, Shinano.

@26072
Tsugawa Yoshifuyu ?-1584

@26073
Oda vassal; lord of Matsugashima Castle, Ise, of the house of the Shiba, Constables of Owari. Served as an elder to Oda Nobukatsu, but was killed through Toyotomi Hideyoshi's scheming.

@26074
Tachihara Hisatsuna 1531-1613

@26075
Amago vassal. With his nephew Yamanaka Shikanosuke he raised Amago Katsuhisa to restore the clan. Captured by the Mori after Kozuki Castle, Harima, fell, he escaped to Kyoto, and later went to Awa.

@26076
Oda Nobutomo ?-1555

@26077
Deputy constable of the four lower districts of Owari; lord of Kiyosu Castle. For killing the constable Shiba Yoshimune, he was attacked at Kiyosu by Nobumitsu on Nobunaga's orders and committed seppuku.

@26078
Oda Nobuhide 1510-1551

@26079
Father of Nobunaga; a fierce general called "the Tiger of Owari". Fought the neighbouring Imagawa, Saito and others to unite Owari, but died of an epidemic with his ambition unfulfilled.

@26080
Tsuda Nobusumi 1558-1582

@26081
Son of Oda Nobuyuki. After his father's death he served his uncle Nobunaga, and married Akechi Mitsuhide's daughter. Praised as "a rare gem", but after Honnoji he was killed by Nobutaka.

@26082
Oda Nobukane 1543-1614

@26083
Younger brother of Nobunaga; fought in Echizen and at the Ishiyama Honganji. After Honnoji he served Toyotomi Hideyoshi as guardian of his son Hideyori. His daughter became Hideyoshi's beloved concubine.

@26084
Kawajiri Hidetaka 1527-1582

@26085
Oda vassal; chief of the Black Cloaks. Became aide to Nobutada, Nobunaga's heir. Won fame destroying the Takeda and was given all of Kai. After Honnoji he was killed in an uprising of the Kai gentry.

@26086
Kawajiri Hidenaga ?-1600

@26087
Toyotomi vassal; lord of Naegi Castle, Mino. Son of Hidetaka, also called Naotsugu. Fought at Komaki and Odawara; was stationed at Nagoya Castle, Hizen, during the Korean campaign. Fell at Sekigahara.

@26088
Shibata Katsutoyo ?-1583

@26089
Shibata vassal; adopted son of his uncle Katsuie. Became lord of Nagahama Castle, Omi, after the Kiyosu council. When he fell out with Katsuie, Hashiba Hideyoshi attacked and he surrendered. Died of illness just after Shizugatake.

@26090
Niwa Nagashige 1571-1637

@26091
Toyotomi vassal; heir of Nagahide. Inherited Kitanosho, Echizen (1.2 million koku) on his father's death. Once cut to Matsuto, Kaga (40,000), he sided with the Tokugawa at Osaka and held Shirakawa, Mutsu (100,000).

@26092
Sakazaki Naomori ?-1616

@26093
Son of Ukita Tadaie. Left the Ukita over a family feud and served Tokugawa Ieyasu. For his service at Sekigahara he held Hamada, Iwami (20,000 koku). At Osaka he rescued Princess Sen, Ieyasu's granddaughter.

@26094
Hiraoka Yorikatsu 1560-1607

@26095
Kobayakawa vassal. At Sekigahara he conspired with Kuroda Nagamasa to make Hideaki attack Otani Yoshitsugu's position, which decided the battle. After Hideaki's death he served Tokugawa Ieyasu.

@26096
Matsuno Shigemoto ?-1655

@26097
Kobayakawa vassal, commonly called "Shume". Won fame as a gunnery captain and was granted the Toyotomi name. When his lord Hideaki betrayed the West at Sekigahara, he refused to follow and left the field.

@26098
Harada Naomasa ?-1576

@26099
Oda vassal; one of the Red Cloaks. Real name Ban Shigetomo. Fought at Nagashino and against the Nagashima Ikko uprising. Fell in a defeat by the Ikko during the siege of the Ishiyama Honganji.
