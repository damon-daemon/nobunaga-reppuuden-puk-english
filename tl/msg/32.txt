Otomo Chikaie 1561-1641

@32001
Sorin's second son. Left the priesthood and was baptised. His lands were seized for dealing secretly with the Shimazu. After the Otomo were stripped of their domain, he served the Tachibana and the Hosokawa.

@32002
Otomo Chikamori 1567-1643

@32003
Sorin's third son, the one his father trusted most. Kept the home province during the Hyuga campaign. Led the vanguard at Hetsugigawa in Bungo, but was beaten. After the Otomo fell he served the Hosokawa.

@32004
Kikuchi Yoshitake 1505-1554

@32005
Brother of Otomo Yoshiaki; succeeded to the Kikuchi, a noble Higo house. Allied with the Ouchi against his brother, but lost. Rose again after the Nikai Kuzure Incident to take Higo, but was killed by Otomo Sorin.

@32006
Yoshihiro Akitada ?-1571

@32007
An Otomo vassal; one of the Three Bungo Elders, he helped the house expand. Won merit in many battles, repelling the Ouchi at Seibagahara and beating the Mori at Tatarahama.

@32008
Yoshihiro Muneyuki 1564-1600

@32009
An Otomo vassal. At Sekigahara he opposed his lord Yoshimune's plan to join the West and urged siding with the East, but was overruled. Followed Yoshimune against Kuroda Kanbei's army and died in battle.

@32010
Takahashi Kanetane ?-1579

@32011
An Otomo vassal; lord of Homan Castle in Chikuzen. Went over to the Mori and Akizuki, but was beaten and returned. After the Otomo defeat at Mimikawa he rebelled again, and died soon after.

@32012
Usuki Akihaya 1538-1574

@32013
An Otomo vassal; one of the Three Bungo Elders, aiding the head Sorin, mainly in diplomacy and government. Commanded the Otomo forces against the Mori army in Chikuzen.

@32014
Yoshioka Nagamasu ?-1573

@32015
An Otomo vassal; one of the Three Bungo Elders, aiding the head Sorin. Stirred Ouchi Teruhiro of Funai to attack Yamaguchi, forcing the Mori army facing them in Chikuzen to withdraw.

@32016
Nyuta Chikazane ?-1550

@32017
An Otomo vassal and Sorin's tutor. Failing to win Sorin's trust, he plotted to have him disinherited. Fled in defeat in the Nikai Kuzure Incident and was later murdered.

@32018
Shiga Chikamori ?-?

@32019
An Otomo vassal; a senior retainer and a pillar of the domain's rule. Camped in Higo with the southern district bands, but his failure to act led to the Otomo army's crushing defeat at Mimikawa.

@32020
Ichimada Akizane ?-1588

@32021
An Otomo vassal. Won much merit in battle, but was also a man of taste who invited his lord Sorin to cherry-blossom viewings and Noh. Killed himself to atone for a traitor among his kin.

@32022
Saeki Korenori ?-1578

@32023
An Otomo vassal. In the Nikai Kuzure Incident he sided with Sorin and worked to put it down. Later served the Saionji of Iyo, then came home. Sent into Hyuga by Sorin, he died at Mimikawa.

@32024
Saeki Koresada 1569-1618

@32025
An Otomo vassal; lord of Togamure Castle in Bungo. Repelled Shimazu Iehisa's invading army with a siege defence and was praised by Toyotomi Hideyoshi. After the Otomo fell he served Todo Takatora.

@32026
Akizuki Tanenaga ?-1614

@32027
Tanezane's eldest son. Submitted to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Kyushu campaign and held Takanabe in Hyuga, 30,000 koku. Joined the West at Sekigahara but secretly went over to the East, and kept his lands.

@32028
Takahashi Mototane 1571-1614

@32029
Akizuki Tanezane's second son. Submitted to Toyotomi Hideyoshi; held Nobeoka in Hyuga, 50,000 koku. Went over to the East at Sekigahara and kept his lands. Stripped of his domain for sheltering a traitor's kin.

@32030
Tsukushi Korekado 1531-1567

@32031
First under the Shoni; when they declined he dealt with the Ouchi and Mori and contested land with the Otomo. Allied with the Akizuki, he resisted Otomo Sorin's attacks, but lost and killed himself.

@32032
Harada Nobutane ?-1598

@32033
Lord of Takasu Castle in Chikuzen, born a Kusano; adopted after Harada Chikatane's death. Submitted to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Kyushu campaign, keeping land in Buzen. Died in the Korean campaign.

@32034
Kamachi Akimori 1519-1578

@32035
Lord of Yanagawa Castle in Chikugo, under the Otomo; a friend of the Ryuzoji. Sheltered Iekane, who escaped a Shoni trap, and Takanobu, driven from Saga Castle in Hizen by his vassals.

@32036
Ryuzoji Nobuchika ?-?

@32037
Chikaie's second son. Followed his brother Takanobu in many campaigns with distinction. As the Otomo declined he pushed into Chikuzen and Buzen, pacifying north Buzen and helping the house expand.

@32038
Ryuzoji Naganobu ?-1603

@32039
Chikaie's third son. Distinguished himself under his eldest brother Takanobu in the conquest of Hizen and led the Hizen local lords. When Nabeshima Naoshige took power, he and his brother Nobuchika submitted to him.

@32040
Ryuzoji Ienari ?-?

@32041
A Ryuzoji vassal of the Muranaka branch. Followed Takanobu of the Mizugae branch and destroyed the Shoni. Later sent to north Higo, he held back the Shimazu army marching north and won merit.

@32042
Nabeshima Katsushige 1580-1657

@32043
Naoshige's heir. Sided with the West at Sekigahara, but switched to the East without fighting in the main battle. Then submitted to Tokugawa Ieyasu and replaced his old lords the Ryuzoji as lord of Saga domain in Hizen.

@32044
Eriguchi Nobutsune ?-1584

@32045
One of the Four Ryuzoji Guardians. Hearing of Takanobu's death at Okitanawate, he charged alone into the Shimazu headquarters. Died a heroic death; the enemy general Shimazu Iehisa praised him as "a true warrior".

@32046
Hyakutake Katakane ?-1584

@32047
One of the Four Ryuzoji Guardians. Shone at Imayama and elsewhere; his valour, said to equal a hundred men, earned him the name Hyakutake ("hundred warriors") from Takanobu. Died guarding Takanobu at Okitanawate.

@32048
Narutomi Shigeyasu 1560-1634

@32049
A Ryuzoji vassal. Won much merit in battles everywhere; even Toyotomi Hideyoshi recognised his valour. Later served the Nabeshima, lords of Saga domain in Hizen, in public works and waterworks.

@32050
Egami Taketane ?-?

@32051
A Shoni vassal; lord of Sefukuji Castle in Hizen, one of the 19 generals of east Hizen. Fought Oda Masamitsu of the Ryuzoji and others. After the Shoni fell he became a vassal of Ryuzoji Takanobu.

@32052
Matsura Takanobu 1529-1599

@32053
Lord of Hirado Castle in Hizen. Expanded in west Hizen, but lost to Ryuzoji Takanobu and submitted. Seeing the value of foreign trade, he made Hirado a major centre of trade with the Europeans.

@32054
Omura Sumiaki 1473-1551

@32055
Pressed by Arima Haruzumi, he adopted Haruzumi's second son Sumitada and gave his own son Takaaki to the Goto for adoption. Making Sumitada his heir provoked his vassals' anger.

@32056
Omura Yoshiaki 1569-1616

@32057
Sumitada's son. Fought for the East at Sekigahara and kept his lands. Converted from Christianity to Buddhism and persecuted Christians; murdered by an unknown hand.

@32058
Arima Haruzumi 1483-1566

@32059
Lord of Hinoe Castle in Hizen. Expanded from Arima in Hizen, building the largest domain the Arima ever had. Ended a long feud by giving his second son Sumitada to the Omura for adoption.

@32060
Arima Yoshisada 1521-1576

@32061
Haruzumi's son. Absorbed in poetry, he lost the lands his father had built to Ryuzoji Takanobu one by one, barely holding on to the area around his seat, Hinoe Castle in Hizen.

@32062
Aso Koretoyo 1493-1559

@32063
Lord of Iwao Castle and chief priest of the Aso Shrine. Fought his brother Korenaga for the office and secured power with the Kai's help. From then on he and the Kai followed the Otomo.

@32064
Uemura Yorioki 1490-1557

@32065
A Sagara vassal; lord of Uemura Castle in Higo. Father of Sagara Haruhiro, he held real power as kin. Stopped at nothing to protect himself, murdering even his brother and brother-in-law.

@32066
Indo Yorimori 1568-1655

@32067
Yoriyasu's son; a Sagara vassal. At Sekigahara he made the house secretly join the East and so survive. Later served as home elder, but his high-handed conduct got him exiled.

@32068
Okamoto Yoriuji 1537-1606

@32069
A Sagara vassal who won merit in 19 battles from his first. Killed Kawakami Hisaaki of the Shimazu at Okuchi. Wrote a record of his service, the "Battlefield Diary of Okamoto Yoriuji".

@32070
Akahoshi Muneie 1530-1619

@32071
An Otomo vassal; Chikaie's son. Lost to Kumabe Chikanaga, a Ryuzoji ally, and surrendered. When his son, sent as a hostage, was killed, he went over to the Shimazu and fought the Ryuzoji.

@32072
Kumabe Chikanaga ?-1588

@32073
One of the Three Kikuchi Elders. Allied with Ryuzoji Takanobu against the Akahoshi and took Waifu Castle. Later served the Shimazu and the Toyotomi; led a local uprising against Sassa Narimasa's rule.

@32074
Jo Chikamoto ?-1592

@32075
An Otomo vassal; Chikafuyu's second son. When the Otomo lost power at Mimikawa, he came under the Shimazu. Left them just before Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Kyushu campaign and submitted to Hideyoshi.

@32076
Ito Sukeyoshi 1589-1636

@32077
Suketaka's son. Fought for the East at Sekigahara, attacking Miyazaki and Sadowara castles in Hyuga. Inherited Obi in Hyuga, 36,000 koku, on his father's death and founded Obi domain; surveyed and cleared land.

@32078
Mera Suketsugu ?-1578

@32079
An Ito vassal; lord of Kadogawa Castle in Hyuga. When the Shimazu advanced into Hyuga he gave hostages and submitted. Secretly sided with Otomo Sorin and fought for the Otomo at Mimikawa, where he died.

@32080
Yamada Muneaki 1544-1620

@32081
An Ito vassal. After the Ito fell he took refuge with Saeki Koresada, an Otomo vassal. When the Shimazu began invading Bungo, he readied Togamure Castle in Bungo for defence and led the whole battle.

@32082
Hongo Tokihisa 1530-1596

@32083
Lord of Shonai in Hyuga. Under the Shimazu he repelled the Ito and Kimotsuki and kept his lands. Submitted to Toyotomi Hideyoshi and kept them; later moved to Miyanojo in Satsuma.

@32084
Nejime Shigetake 1536-1580

@32085
Lord of Nejime in Osumi. Fought the Shimazu as a Kimotsuki ally. Attacked by the Kimotsuki for making a separate peace with the Shimazu, he drove them off with Shimazu help. Traded with Ming China.

@32086
Nejime Shigehira 1566-1629

@32087
A Shimazu vassal; a local lord of Nejime in Osumi. Under the Kimotsuki until his father Shigetake submitted to the Shimazu; served Yoshihisa, Yoshihiro and Iehisa. Moved to Yoshitoshi in Satsuma by Hideyoshi's land survey.

@32088
Shimazu Toshihisa 1537-1592

@32089
Takahisa's third son. Resisted Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Kyushu campaign to the last. When Umekita Kunikane rebelled against Hideyoshi and many of his vassals joined in, he took the blame and killed himself.

@32090
Ei Hisatora 1558-1587

@32091
A Shimazu vassal who won outstanding merit in all the main battles, such as Mimikawa and Okitanawate. Shimazu Yoshihiro is said to have said: "Every victory in Bungo and Hizen was thanks to Hisatora."

@32092
Kabayama Hisataka 1558-1634

@32093
A Shimazu vassal and elder. In Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Korean campaign he defeated Yi Sun-sin's turtle-ship fleet. Later he led an expedition to Ryukyu, took Shuri and captured the prince.

@32094
Kamata Masatoshi 1514-1583

@32095
A Shimazu vassal. Took Magoshi Castle in Satsuma in the fight with Hishikari Takaaki and was praised for it. Later advanced into Higo with Niiro Tadamoto and took the Aso's Yasaki Castle.

@32096
Saruwatari Nobumitsu 1534-1587

@32097
A Shimazu vassal, active mainly in Hizen and Higo. At Okitanawate he helped destroy the Ryuzoji army. Beaten by Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Kyushu campaign army, he died in battle.

@32098
Yamada Arinobu 1544-1609

@32099
A Shimazu vassal. After the Ito fell he became lord of Taka Castle in Hyuga. With a few men he held his castle against the Otomo army, contributing greatly to the Shimazu victory at Mimikawa.
