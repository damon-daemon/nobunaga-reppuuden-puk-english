@18000
Hachisuka Masakatsu 1526-1586

@18001
Toyotomi vassal. Helped build the overnight castle at Sunomata, then excelled as adviser in civil rule and subversion. After the Shikoku campaign held 180,000 koku at Tokushima in Awa to watch the Chosokabe.

@18002
Todo Takatora 1556-1630

@18003
Served seven lords, Tokugawa Ieyasu among them, raising his stipend with each change, and rose to daimyo of 320,000 koku at Anotsu in Ise. A master castle builder who oversaw many castles.

@18004
Konishi Yukinaga 1555-1600

@18005
Toyotomi vassal and skilled naval commander. Fought in Korea and held 240,000 koku at Uto in Higo. Sided with the West at Sekigahara and lost. As a Christian he would not kill himself, and was beheaded.

@18006
Saito Yoshitatsu 1527-1561

@18007
Dosan's son, though some say Toki Yoriyoshi's. Fell out with his father over the succession, killed him and became lord of Mino, but died suddenly. Said to have been a giant over six feet tall.

@18008
Inaba Ittetsu 1515-1588

@18009
Saito vassal; one of the Mino Triumvirate. Served the Oda after his clan fell. At Anegawa struck the Azai in the flank and won the day. His stubbornness gave rise to the word "ittetsu".

@18010
Ishikawa Kazumasa ?-1592

@18011
Tokugawa vassal; an elder who led the western Mikawa men. Fled to the Toyotomi after Komaki-Nagakute, forcing the Tokugawa to remodel their old Mikawa army on Takeda lines.

@18012
Sakai Tadatsugu 1527-1596

@18013
First of Tokugawa's Four Heavenly Kings, once Ieyasu's guardian. After Ieyasu came of age led the eastern Mikawa men on many campaigns and won much glory. Even Toyotomi Hideyoshi praised his ability.

@18014
Honda Masanobu 1538-1616

@18015
Tokugawa vassal. Joined the rebels in the Mikawa Ikko uprising and wandered the land before returning. Showed great skill at administration and intrigue; Ieyasu is said to have called him "friend".

@18016
Hattori Hanzo 1542-1596

@18017
Tokugawa vassal who led the Iga ninja to many victories. Feared as "Demon Hanzo", yet a man of feeling: chosen to assist at Matsudaira Nobuyasu's suicide, he is said to have dropped his sword and wept.

@18018
Date Harumune 1519-1577

@18019
15th head of the Date. Fought his father Tanemune in the Tenbun Rebellion, then took over the clan when it ended. Like his father, made shrewd marriage alliances that secured the Date's standing.

@18020
Date Terumune 1544-1585

@18021
Harumune's second son; fought the Soma. A year after handing the clan to his heir Masamune, was seized by Nihonmatsu (Hatakeyama) Yoshitsugu, and killed in the Date gunfire meant to rescue him.

@18022
Tozawa Moriyasu 1566-1590

@18023
18th head of the Tozawa. In his first battle cut an enemy general in two, earning the name "Demon Kuro". It is said he never took the heads of wounded foes, but let them go.

@18024
Mogami Yoshimori 1521-1590

@18025
10th head of the Mogami; head of the clan at 2. Doted on his second son Yoshitoki and sought to make him heir, but his eldest Yoshiaki, exploiting strife in the Date clan, rose up and forced him to retire.

@18026
Satake Yoshinobu 1570-1633

@18027
19th head of the Satake, a warrior as fierce as his father Yoshishige. Firmly resolved to join the West at Sekigahara, he was called by Tokugawa Ieyasu "a man of rare honesty in this age".

@18028
Nagano Narimasa 1491-1561

@18029
Yamanouchi-Uesugi vassal. Held Minowa Castle in Kozuke after his masters fell, repelling Takeda Shingen six times. A wise general who made Shingen lament, "While Narimasa lives, Kozuke is beyond reach."

@18030
Ota Sukemasa 1522-1591

@18031
Ogigayatsu-Uesugi vassal; lord of Iwatsuki Castle in Musashi. After his masters fell joined Uesugi Kenshin and Satake Yoshishige, fighting the Hojo all his life. Died after seeing Toyotomi Hideyoshi take Odawara.

@18032
Hojo Ujimasa 1538-1590

@18033
4th head of the Hojo. Backed by able brothers and retainers, steadily widened his domain. Besieged at Odawara by Toyotomi Hideyoshi's army, surrendered and killed himself after the war.

@18034
Hojo Ujinao 1562-1591

@18035
5th head of the Hojo. After Honnoji invaded Oda lands, crushed Takigawa Kazumasu and took Kozuke. But beaten by the Toyotomi at Odawara, was confined on Mt. Koya and died there of illness.

@18036
Hojo Tsunashige 1515-1587

@18037
Son of the Imagawa vassal Kushima Masashige. Married Hojo Ujitsuna's daughter and took the Hojo name. Famed for valour, feared as "Yellow Hachiman" after his banner. Lord of Kawagoe at the night battle there.

@18038
Fuma Kotaro ?-?

@18039
Chief of the Soshu rappa, who gathered intelligence and sowed chaos in enemy lands, quietly backing five generations of Hojo rule. Said to stand over seven feet, with slanted eyes and four fangs jutting out.

@18040
Takeda Nobushige 1525-1561

@18041
Shingen's younger brother; skilled in war and letters and well loved, he served as his brother's deputy. Died at Kawanakajima defending Shingen; later praised as "a true warrior".

@18042
Kosaka Masanobu 1527-1578

@18043
One of Takeda's Four Great Retainers, hailed as the clan's finest tactician and nicknamed "Retreating Danjo". As keeper of Kaizu Castle in Shinano, he kept watch on the Uesugi.

@18044
Naito Masatoyo 1522-1575

@18045
One of Takeda's Four Great Retainers. After Takeda Nobushige's death became Shingen's deputy. As lord of Minowa Castle in Kozuke took and held Kanto lands. Killed at Nagashino.

@18046
Sanada Nobuyuki 1566-1658

@18047
Masayuki's eldest son. Married to the daughter of the Tokugawa vassal Honda Tadakatsu, he left his father and brother to join the East at Sekigahara. Stayed loyal to the Tokugawa and kept the Sanada alive.

@18048
Imagawa Ujizane 1538-1614

@18049
Took over after the death of his father Yoshimoto, "the Giant of Tokai". But devoted to kemari and poetry, he idled his days away, and so was driven from his lands by Tokugawa Ieyasu and Takeda Shingen.

@18050
Azai Hisamasa 1525-1573

@18051
Lord of Odani Castle in Omi; in northern Omi stronger than his masters the Kyogoku. When relations with the Kyogoku soured and the Rokkaku of southern Omi pressed him, submitted to them to save his house.

@18052
Miyoshi Yoshikata 1527-1562

@18053
Motonaga's second son; a fine tea master. When his brother Chokei moved into the Kinai, governed their home province of Awa. Chokei's right hand in rule and war; killed at Kumeda in Izumi.

@18054
Takayama Shigetomo 1552-1615

@18055
Oda vassal; fought at Yamazaki and elsewhere. A Christian daimyo who spread the faith from Takatsuki Castle in Settsu. Later banished to Luzon by the Tokugawa ban on Christianity.

@18056
Mori Terumoto 1553-1625

@18057
Takamoto's heir; took over after his grandfather Motonari's death. Joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi and became one of the Five Elders. Nominal head of the West at Sekigahara; cut back to Suo and Nagato.

@18058
Kikkawa Hiroie 1561-1625

@18059
Motoharu's third son. Foreseeing the West's defeat at Sekigahara, tried by diplomacy to save the Mori main line, but failed. The clan's lands were cut, and he was branded a traitor within it.

@18060
Kobayakawa Hideaki 1582-1602

@18061
Younger brother of Kinoshita Katsutoshi; adopted by Kobayakawa Takakage at Toyotomi Hideyoshi's order. Turned to the East at Sekigahara and swung the battle, but tormented by guilt died of illness two years later.

@18062
Ankokuji Ekei ?-1600

@18063
Mori vassal; a monk-diplomat. With rare foresight predicted Oda Nobunaga's fall and Toyotomi Hideyoshi's rise. After Sekigahara beheaded as one of the ringleaders.

@18064
Kuroda Nagamasa 1568-1623

@18065
Kanbei's heir. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi with distinction. Joined the East at Sekigahara and held 520,000 koku at Fukuoka in Chikuzen. Thereafter stayed loyal to the Tokugawa shogunate.

@18066
Ukita Hideie 1572-1655

@18067
Naoie's heir. Favoured by Toyotomi Hideyoshi from childhood, became one of the Five Elders. Led the West's main force at Sekigahara and was exiled to Hachijo Island after the war.

@18068
Chosokabe Kunichika 1504-1560

@18069
Lord of Oko Castle in Tosa. After his father Kanetsugu's death fled to Ichijo Fusaie, who helped him regain Oko. Gave his life to restoring the Chosokabe through marriage ties and building wealth.

@18070
Kira Chikasada 1541-1576

@18071
Second son of Chosokabe Kunichika; a bold and clever general, his brother Motochika's right hand in uniting Tosa. Made keeper of Nakamura Castle in Tosa after the Ichijo fell, but soon died of illness.

@18072
Kosokabe Chikayasu 1543-1593

@18073
Third son of Chosokabe Kunichika. As his brother Motochika's right hand, beat Sogo Nagayasu at Nakatomigawa in Awa and helped unite Shikoku. Also handled diplomacy, going as envoy to the Oda and others.

@18074
Chosokabe Morichika 1575-1615

@18075
Motochika's fourth son. Lost his lands for joining the West at Sekigahara and taught at a temple school in Kyoto. Later hastened to Osaka Castle and fought well in the Summer Campaign; beheaded after its fall.

@18076
Kai Soun 1515-1584

@18077
Aso vassal; real name Chikanao. While under the Otomo, kept his lands by shrewd dealings with the Ryuzoji and Shimazu. Fought Sagara Yoshihi, then on the Shimazu side, who died in defeat.

@18078
Akizuki Fumitane ?-1557

@18079
Lord of Kosho Castle in Chikuzen. Served the Otomo but, unhappy with his treatment, rose against them in league with Mori Motonari. Fought well against 20,000 Otomo troops but lost and killed himself.

@18080
Akizuki Tanezane 1548-1596

@18081
Fumitane's eldest son. With Mori help retook Kosho Castle in Chikuzen from the Otomo. Sided with the Shimazu and lost to Toyotomi Hideyoshi; offered the famed Narashiba tea caddy to save his house.

@18082
Tsukushi Hirokado 1556-1623

@18083
Lord of Yamashita Castle in Chikuzen. Like his father Korekado, who served the Shoni, Otomo and Mori in turn, served the Otomo, Shimazu and Toyotomi to keep his house alive. Lost his lands for joining the West.

@18084
Omura Sumitada 1533-1587

@18085
Arima Haruzumi's second son; adopted by Omura Sumiaki, whom he succeeded. Opened Nagasaki as a port for Portuguese trade. Japan's first Christian daimyo; sent a boys' embassy to Rome.

@18086
Arima Harunobu 1567-1612

@18087
Yoshisada's second son. Allied with the Shimazu and killed Ryuzoji Takanobu at Okitanawate. Bribed Okamoto Daihachi, Honda Masazumi's man, to regain old lands; when found out, was put to death.

@18088
Shimazu Takahisa 1514-1571

@18089
Eldest son of Tadayoshi of a Shimazu branch; adopted by Katsuhisa, became 16th head. Crushed rivals to unify his lands, and laid the base of the Shimazu's power with guns and bold diplomacy.

@18090
Shimazu Tadatsune 1578-1638

@18091
Yoshihiro's third son. Won honour in Korea with his father. Took over the clan after Sekigahara and made Ryukyu a vassal of Satsuma. Rebuilt Tsurumaru Castle and laid out its town.

@18092
Uwai Kakuken 1545-1589

@18093
Shimazu vassal. Fought on many fronts and helped run the domain as senior councillor. A man of culture, gifted in letters, poetry and tea, who wrote the "Precepts for the Governor of Ise" and a diary.

@18094
Wada Koremasa 1532-1571

@18095
Ashikaga vassal. After Yoshiteru's death served Yoshiaki, quietly helping him become Shogun. Protected Christianity and introduced the missionary Luis Frois to Oda Nobunaga.

@18096
Oda Urakusai 1547-1621

@18097
Nobunaga's younger brother; real name Nagamasu. A monk devoted to tea, one of Rikyu's Seven Disciples. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi, but at Osaka secretly sided with the Tokugawa and left the castle.

@18098
Akita Sanesue 1576-1659

@18099
Ando Chikasue's heir; took over at 12 on his father's death. Fought the Nanbu and Onodera and held his lands. After a misstep at Sekigahara he was driven from Akita.
