@35000
The turmoil that began with the Onin War spread ever wider. Takeda Harunobu (Shingen) had seized the headship by banishing his father Nobutora. Saito Dosan had risen from oil seller to lord of Mino in one lifetime. Imagawa Yoshimoto held Suruga and Totomi and had brought the Matsudaira of Mikawa under his wing.

@35001
As daimyo everywhere rose to claim supremacy, Kipposhi, heir of Oda Nobuhide of Owari, came of age and took the name Nobunaga. Born amid the age of war, a fierce wind began to blow, destined to end the strife.

@35002
In an age when the low overthrew the high, daimyo rose up everywhere. Amid this, Oda Nobuhide, who had been striving to unite Owari, died of illness. His heir Nobunaga, an 18-year-old known as "the Fool", took his place.

@35003
The anti-Nobuhide forces in Owari grew bold, and some uneasy vassals even plotted to set up his brother Nobuyuki instead. East of Owari, Imagawa Yoshimoto awaited his chance to invade. Nobunaga set out to finish what his father never could: the unification of Owari.

@35004
The wars raged on without end. The Ashikaga shoguns had no power to restore order; the Muromachi shogunate struggled merely to survive in the Kinai.

@35005
Meanwhile, daimyo grown strong by conquering their neighbours meant to march on Kyoto and seize the realm. One was Imagawa Yoshimoto of Suruga, "the Finest Bowman of the Tokaido", who led 40,000 men from Suruga, Totomi and Mikawa toward Kyoto. In his path lay Owari, ruled by Oda Nobunaga.

@35006
Ashikaga Yoshiaki became shogun with Oda Nobunaga's backing. Yet fearing the Oda's growing power, Yoshiaki secretly worked to encircle Nobunaga: the Azai and Asakura, beaten by the Oda at Anegawa, the Ikko sect, Enryakuji on Mt. Hiei. He even urged Takeda Shingen, the Tiger of Kai, to march on Kyoto.

@35007
Then in the 9th month the Ikko abbot Honganji Kennyo, pressed by Nobunaga for war funds and to quit Ishiyama, ordered the faithful everywhere to bring Nobunaga down. As the Ikko struck at the Oda, the Kinai was drawn into war once more.

@35008
The Dragon of Echigo, Uesugi Kenshin, was on the move at last. His fated foe Takeda Shingen had died of illness, and Shingen's heir Katsuyori, crushed by the Oda and Tokugawa at Nagashino, had lost his momentum.

@35009
Allied with the Ikko, Kenshin answered Shogun Yoshiaki's call to encircle Oda Nobunaga and pushed west. Nobunaga marched east to meet him, and the two armies faced each other across the Tedori River. The first and last clash between the age's greatest warrior and its supreme overlord was about to begin.

@35010
The Oda domain had swollen to 21 provinces, and Nobunaga's rule of the realm by arms was within reach. The Kinai was wholly Oda land, while Shibata Katsuie's corps in Hokuriku and Hashiba Hideyoshi's corps in Chugoku pressed on with the conquest.

@35011
In the 2nd month of 1582, seeing every front going well, Nobunaga judged the time had come to destroy the Takeda, and sent his main army into Takeda lands under his heir Nobutada. The fall of the Takeda was only a matter of time. But what fate awaited beyond that, Nobunaga could not yet know.

@35012
On the brink of uniting the realm, Oda Nobunaga fell with his ambition unfulfilled. Akechi Mitsuhide's rebellion succeeded, but he was struck down before he could seize the realm, and the war flared again. Who would be the new wind to quell the flames?

@35013
Hashiba Hideyoshi, who had avenged Nobunaga and held Nobutada's orphan Sanboshi, or Shibata Katsuie, first of the Oda vassals, backing Nobunaga's third son Nobutaka? The Oda were about to split over Nobunaga's succession, and with it, the realm.

@35014
After Honnoji, Hashiba Hideyoshi destroyed Akechi Mitsuhide and Shibata Katsuie and was securing his place as Nobunaga's successor. Wary of him, Nobunaga's second son Nobukatsu joined Tokugawa Ieyasu, lord of five provinces, and stirred anti-Hideyoshi forces everywhere to threaten his rear.

@35015
Hideyoshi answered with a war of diplomacy, and the rift spread across the land. In the 3rd month of 1584, Nobukatsu and Ieyasu went to war and camped on Mt. Komaki. The clash of two great men, Hideyoshi and Ieyasu, was about to begin.

@35016
Having routed the Otomo at Mimikawa and slain Ryuzoji Takanobu at Okitanawate, the Shimazu marched north to conquer Kyushu, driving Otomo Sorin back into Bungo. Helpless before them, Sorin begged Toyotomi Hideyoshi, now nearly master of the realm, for aid.

@35017
Hideyoshi ordered the Shimazu to make peace, but Shimazu Yoshihisa pressed his attack, hoping to take Kyushu before Hideyoshi's army arrived. At last Hideyoshi resolved to invade, ordering the daimyo of Chugoku and Shikoku to lead the vanguard.

@35018
Granted the name "Toyotomi" by the Court, Hideyoshi had become Regent and Chancellor. Having made vassals of the Chosokabe, the Shimazu and the Tokugawa, he was master of the realm in name and fact, and forbade private wars between daimyo.

@35019
In 1589, when Hojo vassals defied this order and invaded Kozuke, Hideyoshi resolved to crush the defiant Hojo and launched the Odawara campaign, leading a great army himself. The age of war that had seemed eternal was drawing to a close.

@35020
In the late 16th century, heroes revived from ages past descended upon a Japan torn by war: great generals of the Three Kingdoms, champions of the Mongol Empire, the warriors who founded the Kamakura shogunate. Heroes whose names shine like stars in history rose to claim the realm, eager to win glory once more.

@35021
The daimyo and the heroes of old... Who would rule the realm? The land of the warring states was gripped by tension as never before.
