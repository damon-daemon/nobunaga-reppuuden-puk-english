@19000
Ikeda Tsuneoki 1536-1584

@19001
Oda vassal and Nobunaga's milk brother. Shone at Anegawa and elsewhere. After Honnoji became one of the Oda's four senior elders. Sided with Hashiba Hideyoshi at Komaki-Nagakute and was killed there.

@19002
Ikeda Terumasa 1564-1613

@19003
Tsuneoki's second son. Served Oda Nobunaga, then Toyotomi Hideyoshi, in many battles. Joined the East at Sekigahara and received 520,000 koku at Himeji in Harima, called "the Himeji Councillor".

@19004
Hori Hidemasa 1553-1590

@19005
Oda Nobunaga's close aide. Won many battles and also hosted Tokugawa Ieyasu, showing talent in both war and peace. After Nobunaga served Toyotomi Hideyoshi, treated as one of his kin.

@19006
Kato Yoshiakira 1563-1631

@19007
Toyotomi vassal; a calm, brave man and one of the Seven Spears of Shizugatake. Led the Toyotomi navy in many battles. Joined the East at Sekigahara and held 200,000 koku at Matsuyama in Iyo.

@19008
Katagiri Katsumoto 1556-1615

@19009
One of the Seven Spears of Shizugatake. Made guardian of Toyotomi Hideyori, strove to save the Toyotomi. Suspected of dealings with Tokugawa Ieyasu, left Osaka Castle. Died in Kyoto after its fall.

@19010
Okubo Tadayo 1532-1594

@19011
Tokugawa vassal, serving since before Ieyasu won independence; fought in many campaigns with great merit. Austere and unbending, yet generous enough to plead for retainers who had fled to be taken back.

@19012
Utsunomiya Kunitsuna 1568-1607

@19013
Hirotsuna's heir. Was quick to join Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara and was confirmed in his lands. But in 1597 he was caught up in a Toyotomi power struggle and stripped of them.

@19014
Haga Takasada ?-?

@19015
Utsunomiya vassal. Replaced the rebellious Haga Takatsune and Takateru as head of the Haga. Aided the boy lord Hirotsuna as senior elder in government, wielding immense power in the clan.

@19016
Masaki Tokishige ?-1573

@19017
Satomi vassal. A fine spearman, called "Spear Daizen". Fought in nearly all the Satomi's battles, the two at Konodai among them, and his valour was known far and wide.

@19018
Hojo Ujiteru 1540-1590

@19019
Ujiyasu's second son; aided his brother Ujimasa in war and diplomacy. Active in the east of the Hojo lands, took Shimotsuke and Shimosa. After Odawara fell, killed himself with his brother on Toyotomi Hideyoshi's order.

@19020
Anegakoji Yoritsuna 1540-1587

@19021
Yoshiyori's eldest son. Defeated the local lords and unified Hida. After Honnoji joined Sassa Narimasa against Hashiba Hideyoshi but lost. Died in Kyoto, where he lived in retirement.

@19022
Maeda Keiji 1540-1612

@19023
Born a Takigawa, adopted by Toshihisa, elder brother of Maeda Toshiie. When Toshiie became head of the Maeda, left as a ronin and served the Uesugi as "Kokuzoin Hyottokosai". A ronin again after Sekigahara.

@19024
Hatakeyama Takamasa 1527-1576

@19025
Constable of Kawachi; warred and made peace with the Miyoshi by turns. Later submitted to Oda Nobunaga and was given half of Kawachi, but lost a feud with his vassal Yusa Nobunori and became a wanderer.

@19026
Sogo Nagayasu 1554-1586

@19027
Son of Miyoshi Yoshikata; adopted by his uncle Sogo Kazunaga. Beaten by Chosokabe Motochika, fled to Osaka. Joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Shikoku campaign and regained Sanuki. Killed at Hetsugigawa.

@19028
Tawara Chikakata ?-1600

@19029
Of Otomo Sorin's kin; one of his close aides, he took part in government. Backing Sorin, forced through the Hyuga expedition as commander-in-chief, but could not control the army and lost to the Shimazu.

@19030
Kita Nobuchika 1523-1613

@19031
Nanbu vassal. After Harumasa's death backed Nobunao and made him head of the clan, becoming a pillar of the Nanbu. Said to have gone into battle with a Kannon image hidden in his topknot as a charm.

@19032
Kimotsuki Kanetsugu 1511-1566

@19033
Lord of Koyama Castle in Osumi; built the Kimotsuki's widest domain. Married Shimazu Tadayoshi's daughter, but turned against Tadayoshi's son Takahisa. On hearing his castle had fallen to Takahisa, killed himself.

@19034
Oda Nobutada 1557-1582

@19035
Nobunaga's heir. Commanded in quelling Matsunaga Hisahide's revolt and in subduing Kai. Given the headship by Nobunaga, ruled Mino and Owari. Killed himself at Nijo Castle during the Honnoji Incident.

@19036
Oda Nobukatsu 1558-1630

@19037
Nobunaga's second son. Adopted by Kitabatake Tomofusa, Governor of Ise, and became head of the house. Submitted to the Toyotomi after Nobunaga's death, but often rebelled, bringing on Komaki-Nagakute.

@19038
Oda Nobutaka 1558-1583

@19039
Nobunaga's third son; adopted by Kanbe Tomomori of Ise and became his heir. After Honnoji joined Shibata Katsuie against Hashiba Hideyoshi. Beaten by his brother Nobukatsu and forced to kill himself.

@19040
Oda Nobuyuki ?-1557

@19041
Nobunaga's younger brother. Unlike Nobunaga, called a fool, he was bright from a child and well regarded. Put forward as the next head by Hayashi Michikatsu and others, but murdered by Nobunaga at Kiyosu Castle.

@19042
Hirate Masahide 1492-1553

@19043
Oda Nobunaga's tutor; arranged the marriage between the Oda and the Saito. Made his own death a final rebuke, reforming the arrogant, wilful Nobunaga and fulfilling his duty.

@19044
Hayashi Michikatsu ?-?

@19045
The Oda's chief elder. Sided with Nobuyuki in the succession dispute but was forgiven. Yet as he did nothing of note thereafter, the old affair was raised again and he was banished.

@19046
Sakuma Nobumori 1527-1582

@19047
Oda vassal; served Nobuhide and Nobunaga in many battles. Given command of the siege of Ishiyama Honganji, he was rebuked for sloth and blunders and expelled from the clan.

@19048
Murai Sadakatsu ?-1582

@19049
Oda vassal. Made Deputy of Kyoto when Nobunaga entered the capital, keeping order there. The clan's finest official, he also helped build Azuchi Castle. Killed at Nijo Castle during the Honnoji Incident.

@19050
Mori Yoshinari 1523-1570

@19051
Oda vassal; shone in the unification of Owari and at Okehazama. Held Usayama Castle in Omi; sallied out to stop the Azai-Asakura advance but was killed under a fierce attack.

@19052
Mori Ranmaru 1565-1582

@19053
Yoshinari's third son; served Oda Nobunaga as a page. Clever and handsome, he was loved by Nobunaga for his talent. A youth of great promise, he died fighting at Honnoji.

@19054
Soma Yoshitane 1548-1635

@19055
Moritane's heir; became 16th head of the Soma when his father retired. Joined the West at Sekigahara and nearly lost his lands, but kept them through Date Masamune's intercession.

@19056
Toyotomi Hideyori 1593-1615

@19057
Toyotomi Hideyoshi's second son; head of the clan at 7. Lost his supremacy at Sekigahara. At Osaka, overprotected by his mother Lady Yodo and others, never once took the field, and killed himself.

@19058
Asano Nagamasa 1547-1611

@19059
Toyotomi vassal. Showed skill as an official as magistrate of Kyoto and became first of the Five Magistrates. After Hideyoshi died, fell out with Ishida Mitsunari, drew close to Tokugawa Ieyasu and served him thereafter.

@19060
Yamanouchi Kazutoyo 1546-1605

@19061
Served Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi. At Sekigahara offered his Kakegawa Castle in Totomi to Tokugawa Ieyasu; helped by his wife Chiyo, he rose to 240,000 koku at Kochi in Tosa.

@19062
Ban Naoyuki 1567-1615

@19063
Toyotomi vassal. Served Kato Yoshiakira as captain of guns, but became a ronin after breaking orders at Sekigahara. Killed in the Osaka Summer Campaign fighting the Asano, vassals of the Tokugawa.

@19064
Kani Saizo 1554-1613

@19065
Samurai general of the Fukushima; learned the spear from Hozoin Inei. At Sekigahara marked all 17 heads he took with bamboo leaves, earning the name "Saizo of the Bamboo".

@19066
Maeda Geni 1539-1602

@19067
A monk of Enryakuji who returned to lay life to serve Oda Nobutada. At Honnoji escaped with Nobutada's heir Hidenobu. Later served Toyotomi Hideyoshi and became one of the Five Magistrates.

@19068
Watanabe Kanbee 1562-1640

@19069
Toyotomi vassal. First into Yamanaka Castle in Izu in the siege of Odawara, his valour was praised by Hideyoshi. At Sekigahara held Koriyama Castle in Yamato. Became a ronin after Osaka.

@19070
Osaki Yoshitaka 1548-1603

@19071
Yoshinao's son. Once repulsed Date Masamune, but later came under his command. Arriving late to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara, lost his lands and served Uesugi Kagekatsu.

@19072
Mori Katsunaga 1577-1615

@19073
Toyotomi vassal. Exiled to Tosa for joining the West at Sekigahara, escaped to Osaka Castle. Respected second only to Sanada Yukimura, fought well in the Summer Campaign and killed himself as the castle fell.

@19074
Kimura Shigenari 1595-1615

@19075
Toyotomi vassal, said to be tall and handsome. Fought his first battle in the Osaka Winter Campaign and was the Toyotomi envoy in the peace with the Tokugawa. Killed in the Summer Campaign.

@19076
Nihonmatsu Yoshitsugu 1552-1585

@19077
Lord of Nihonmatsu Castle in Oshu. Attacked by Date Masamune, surrendered, but fearing for his life fled with Masamune's father Terumune as hostage. Unable to escape, was shot dead along with Terumune.

@19078
Saito Tatsuoki 1548-1573

@19079
Yoshitatsu's heir; took over on his father's death. Betrayed by the Mino Triumvirate and other senior retainers, he was beaten by Oda Nobunaga and driven from Mino. Lived off the Asakura; killed at Tonezaka in Echizen.

@19080
Edo Shigemichi 1556-1598

@19081
Lord of Mito Castle in Hitachi; joined Uesugi Kenshin against the Hojo. Having failed to join Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara, he was attacked by the Satake, lost his castle and fled to the Yuki.

@19082
Ando Morinari ?-1582

@19083
Saito vassal; one of the Mino Triumvirate. Became an Oda vassal after his clan fell. Banished for secret dealings with the Takeda, rose in arms after Honnoji and died fighting Inaba Ittetsu.

@19084
Ujiie Bokuzen ?-1571

@19085
Saito vassal; one of the Mino Triumvirate. Served the Oda after his clan fell. Led the rearguard when Nobunaga's attack on the Nagashima Ikko failed, and was killed in an ambush by the rebels.

@19086
Matsudaira Nobuyasu 1559-1579

@19087
Tokugawa Ieyasu's heir; bold and brilliant, the hope of his father and retainers. But Oda Nobunaga suspected him of dealings with the Takeda, and he killed himself on his father's order, aged 21.

@19088
Yuki Hideyasu 1574-1607

@19089
Tokugawa Ieyasu's second son; adopted by the Toyotomi, then the Yuki. Said to have most inherited his father's fiery temper. Never became Shogun; founded the Echizen Matsudaira.

@19090
Tokugawa Hidetada 1579-1632

@19091
Ieyasu's third son. Held up by the Sanada on the Nakasendo on his way to Sekigahara, angering his father. Yet his father trusted his plain, earnest nature, and he became the second Tokugawa Shogun.

@19092
Torii Mototada 1539-1600

@19093
Tokugawa vassal. At Ieyasu's order held Fushimi Castle before Sekigahara; after 13 days of fighting the Toyotomi, died with all the garrison. His loyalty was praised as "the model of Mikawa samurai".

@19094
Hiraiwa Chikayoshi 1542-1611

@19095
Served Tokugawa Ieyasu from his days as a hostage. Gentle and sincere, enjoyed Ieyasu's full trust and tutored his eldest son Matsudaira Nobuyasu. After Nobuyasu's suicide tutored the eighth son, Yoshinao.

@19096
Honda Masazumi 1565-1637

@19097
Masanobu's heir. Served Tokugawa Ieyasu as an elder at Sunpu Castle. With Konchiin Suden handled domestic and foreign affairs and held 150,000 koku at Utsunomiya, but angered Hidetada and lost his lands.

@19098
Hasekura Tsunenaga 1571-1622

@19099
Date vassal. As a member of the Keicho embassy to Europe, visited Spain and Rome. Came home with little achieved and died in despair two years later.
