@62000
Zhang Fei 167-221

@62001
One of Shu Han's Five Tiger Generals; sworn brother of Liu Bei and Guan Yu. At Changban he alone held Changban Bridge against Cao Cao's vast pursuing army. Styled Yide in the official history.

@62002
Guan Yu 162-219

@62003
First of Shu Han's Five Tiger Generals; sworn brother of Liu Bei and Zhang Fei. Said to be a match for 10,000 men. Held Jing Province after Liu Bei entered Shu; captured and killed by Wu.

@62004
Lu Bu ?-198

@62005
Han general, the fierce warrior of the saying "Lu Bu among men, Red Hare among horses". Rampaged across the land, betraying lord after lord, until his own men betrayed him to a wretched end.

@62006
Ma Chao 176-225

@62007
Ma Teng's eldest son, called "Splendid Ma Chao". Led the Xiliang troops against Chang'an to avenge his father on Cao Cao, but lost. Later joined Liu Bei; one of the Five Tiger Generals.

@62008
Yuan Shao ?-202

@62009
Of the noble Yuan house; leader of the coalition against Dong Zhuo. After Dong Zhuo's death fought Cao Cao for the Central Plain, but his indecision cost him a crushing defeat at Guandu.

@62010
Xiahou Yuan ?-219

@62011
Wei general; Xiahou Dun's cousin, a pillar of Cao Cao's army from the start. A master of the swift strike; misled by Shu strategist Fa Zheng's ploy at Mt. Dingjun, he was cut down by Huang Zhong.

@62012
Xiahou Dun ?-220

@62013
Wei general, kin of Cao Cao. Lost his left eye to Cao Xing's arrow fighting Lu Bu, yet went on leading armies on many fronts. Died of illness soon after Cao Cao, as if following his lord.

@62014
Zhao Yun ?-228

@62015
One of Shu Han's Five Tiger Generals. Served Yuan Shao and Gongsun Zan, then joined Liu Bei. At Changban he rode alone into a vast army and rescued Liu Bei's son A Dou (later Liu Shan).

@62016
Xu Chu ?-?

@62017
Wei general, a giant 8 chi tall. Fought Dian Wei to a draw, won Cao Cao's respect and became his bodyguard. Dull in daily life but fierce in battle, hence his nickname "Mad Tiger".

@62018
Dian Wei ?-197

@62019
Cao Cao's general, likened to the ancient hero E Lai. When Zhang Xiu attacked Wancheng by night, he held off the enemy alone so Cao Cao could escape, and died fighting.

@62020
Zhang Liao 165-224

@62021
Wei general. Fought on many fronts under Lu Bu, then served Cao Cao after Lu Bu's death. Defending Hefei, he drove off 100,000 Wu troops with a mere 800 men.

@62022
Wei Yan ?-234

@62023
Shu Han general. Left Liu Biao for Liu Bei; a fierce warrior second only to the Five Tigers. After Zhuge Liang's death he clashed with Yang Yi and was cut down by Ma Dai on Kongming's secret orders.

@62024
Gan Ning ?-222

@62025
Wu general, once a river pirate of the Brocade Sails. Neglected under Huang Zu, he joined Sun Quan with Su Fei's help. Won Sun Quan's trust fighting Huang Zu, at Red Cliffs and against Wei.

@62026
Huang Gai ?-?

@62027
Wu general, a veteran since Sun Jian's uprising, wielder of an iron whip. At Red Cliffs he and Zhou Yu carried out the "self-injury ruse": feigning surrender, he set Cao Cao's fleet ablaze for Wu.

@62028
Taishi Ci 169-209

@62029
Wu general. At his mother's urging he saved Kong Rong, Governor of Beihai. Served Liu Yao, then joined Sun Ce when Liu Yao fell to him. Died attacking Hefei under Sun Quan.

@62030
Huang Zhong 148-222

@62031
Shu Han general. Served Liu Biao and Han Xuan; under Han Xuan he fought a fierce duel with Guan Yu. Excelled leading troops for Liu Bei; one of the Five Tigers when Liu Bei became King of Hanzhong.

@62032
Zhang He ?-231

@62033
Wei general. Under Yuan Shao, he was cornered by Guo Tu's scheming and went over to Cao Cao with Gao Lan. Fought mainly against Shu Han; at Jieting he surrounded and crushed Ma Su.

@62034
Ma Teng ?-211

@62035
Han Governor of Xiliang, descendant of the early Han general Ma Yuan. Joined Dong Cheng's plot to kill Cao Cao; summoned to the capital, he plotted again, was found out and executed.

@62036
Pang De ?-219

@62037
Wei general. Served Ma Chao, but fell ill while Ma Chao was with Zhang Lu, which parted them. Later served Cao Cao and fought valiantly against Guan Yu.

@62038
Meng Huo ?-?

@62039
King of the Nanman. Rebelled against Shu after Liu Bei's death and fought Zhuge Liang's expedition. Captured seven times and released seven times, at the seventh he submitted sincerely.

@62040
Lu Meng 178-219

@62041
Wu general. A mere warrior until Sun Quan urged him to study; he grew into a general of wit and valour. As Wu's commander he retook Jing Province and captured Guan Yu.

@62042
Liu Bei 161-223

@62043
First emperor of Shu Han, said to descend from Liu Sheng, Prince Jing of Zhongshan. Raised volunteers against the Yellow Turbans, fought far and wide and won a third of China. Emperor Zhaolie.

@62044
Xu Huang 169-228

@62045
Wei general. Served Yang Feng, then joined Cao Cao when Man Chong won him over. Wise and brave, a mainstay of Cao Cao's army on many fronts. Wei's General Who Pacifies the South.

@62046
Xu Shu ?-?

@62047
Disciple of Sima Hui, a skilled swordsman. Served Liu Bei as strategist until Cao Cao seized his mother, forcing him to serve Cao Cao. Recommended Zhuge Liang, the "Sleeping Dragon", to Liu Bei.

@62048
Jia Xu ?-223

@62049
Wei minister. Served Li Jue, was spurned and became Zhang Xiu's adviser; his sharp counsel beat Cao Cao's army again and again. Later went over to Cao Cao with Zhang Xiu. Wei's Grand Commandant.

@62050
Guo Jia 170-207

@62051
Cao Cao's adviser, trusted for his keen wit and judgement. Fell ill during the conquest of Hebei and died aged 38. After Red Cliffs Cao Cao lamented, "If only Guo Jia were here."

@62052
Xun Yu 163-212

@62053
Cao Cao's adviser, a sage Cao Cao called "my Zifang" (Zhang Liang, great strategist of the early Han). Fell out of favour for opposing Cao Cao becoming Duke of Wei, and took his own life.

@62054
Zhuge Liang 181-234

@62055
Chancellor of Shu Han, the "Sleeping Dragon". Won by Liu Bei's three visits, he showed rare gifts in diplomacy and government. Led six northern campaigns; died at Wuzhang Plains. Marquis Zhongwu.

@62056
Zhuge Jin 174-241

@62057
Wu minister, Zhuge Liang's elder brother. Entered service on Lu Su's recommendation. Generous, gentle and sincere, deeply trusted by Sun Quan; worked to keep Shu Han and Wu on good terms.

@62058
Sima Yi 179-251

@62059
Wei minister. Served four generations of the Cao and laid the foundations of the Jin. Won against Shu Han by staying on the defensive. Beat Cao Shuang to seize power in Wei. Later named Emperor Xuan.

@62060
Cao Cao 155-220

@62061
King of Wei. Made his name fighting the Yellow Turbans as a cavalry commandant. After Dong Zhuo's death he swept across the chaotic land and at last became King of Wei. Later named Emperor Wu.

@62062
Sun Jian 156-192

@62063
Said to descend from Sun Tzu. Rose fighting the Yellow Turbans; became Governor of Changsha. Led the van of the coalition against Dong Zhuo. Killed at Xiangyang fighting Yuan Shu and Liu Biao.

@62064
Sun Ce 175-200

@62065
Sun Jian's eldest son. Rose with 3,000 troops borrowed from Yuan Shu and soon conquered all Jiangdong: the "Little Conqueror". Ambushed while hunting, he died young of his wounds.

@62066
Sun Quan 182-252

@62067
First emperor of Wu, Sun Jian's second son. Succeeded when his brother Sun Ce died suddenly. Beat Cao Cao's great host at Red Cliffs. Took the throne in 229 and founded Wu. Emperor Da of Wu.

@62068
Dong Zhuo ?-192

@62069
Han provincial inspector. Answered He Jin's summons to the capital and seized power in the chaos. Deposed an emperor, moved the capital to Chang'an; a tyrant killed by Wang Yun and Lu Bu.

@62070
Liu Shan 207-271

@62071
Second emperor of Shu Han, mild and mediocre. In later years he sank into wine and women and favoured the eunuch Huang Hao, helping to ruin Shu Han. His child name "A Dou" means a fool.

@62072
Jiang Wei 206-264

@62073
Shu Han general. Served Wei, but surrendered to Shu Han in the first northern campaign. The dying Zhuge Liang passed him his art of war. His nine northern campaigns drained the country.

@62074
Zhou Yu 175-210

@62075
Wu general, sworn brother of Sun Ce. Following Sun Ce's last wish he aided young Sun Quan; at Red Cliffs he led the navy as commander and crushed Cao Cao's host. So handsome he was called "Fair Zhou".

@62076
Zhang Zhao 156-236

@62077
Sun Quan's adviser, one of the "Two Zhangs". Invited onto Sun Ce's staff on Zhou Yu's advice. Dying, Sun Ce said: "In home affairs, ask Zhang Zhao in everything."

@62078
Chen Gong ?-198

@62079
Han magistrate of Zhongmou. Fled with Cao Cao after his failed attempt on Dong Zhuo, but left him, appalled by his ruthlessness. Later Lu Bu's adviser; captured in defeat, he asked to be executed.

@62080
Pang Tong 178-213

@62081
Liu Bei's adviser. Studied with Zhuge Liang; the two were called the Sleeping Dragon and the Fledgling Phoenix. Led Liu Bei's conquest of Shu, but was struck by an arrow and died in battle.

@62082
Lu Xun 183-245

@62083
Wu general, Sun Ce's son-in-law. Plotted with Lu Meng to defeat Guan Yu. As grand commander at Yiling he crushed Shu Han's great army. Guarded Jing Province and later became Wu's chancellor.

@62084
Lu Su 172-217

@62085
Wu minister, recommended to Sun Quan by Zhou Yu. At Red Cliffs he urged an alliance with Liu Bei for the decisive battle. Became commander after Zhou Yu's death. Skilled in land warfare.

@62086
Sima Yan 236-290

@62087
First emperor of Jin, Sima Zhao's eldest son. Forced Cao Huan of Wei to abdicate and took the throne. On Yang Hu and Du Yu's advice he conquered Wu, took Sun Hao's surrender and united China. Emperor Wu.

@62088
Cao Bao ?-196

@62089
Tao Qian's officer. When Cao Cao invaded Xu Province he duelled Xiahou Dun, but a fierce wind blew up and he withdrew. Later served Liu Bei, but quarrelled with Zhang Fei and was cut down.
