@23000
Oda Hidenobu 1580-1605

@23001
Heir of Nobutada. After Honnoji, Hashiba Hideyoshi took him in and he headed the Oda at age 3, but Hideyoshi seized real power. Sided with the West at Sekigahara, lost, and became a monk on Mt. Koya.

@23002
Togawa Hideyasu 1533-1592

@23003
Ukita vassal, one of the three Ukita elders. Followed his lord Naoie in many battles; served as chief elder until he retired and passed the headship to his heir Michiyasu.

@23004
Kono Michinobu ?-1581

@23005
Lord of Yuzuki Castle, Iyo; succeeded when his brother Harumichi died young. Struggled with revolts by his vassals at home and invasions by the Mori, Otomo and Chosokabe from abroad.

@23006
Narimatsu Nobukatsu ?-1584

@23007
One of the Four Guardians of the Ryuzoji. Took the head of the Otomo commander at Imayama and was commended by Takanobu. On learning that Takanobu fell at Okitanawate, he died fighting after him.

@23008
Ando Chikasue 1539-1587

@23009
Heir of Kiyosue. United the Minato and Hiyama Ando houses and held his clan together. His skilled strategy built the Ando's largest domain; feared as "like the Dipper in the northern sky".

@23010
Makabe Ujimoto 1550-1622

@23011
Lord of Makabe Castle, Hitachi. Showed his prowess swinging a wooden staff 3 m long instead of sword or spear; feared as "Demon Makabe". Later joined the Satake and fought the Hojo.

@23012
Okubo Tadachika 1553-1628

@23013
Son of Tadayo. Served as elder to Hidetada, third son of Tokugawa Ieyasu. Alone backed Hidetada as heir while most backed Yuki Hideyasu. Later clashed with Honda Masanobu and was attainted.

@23014
Kamei Korenori 1557-1612

@23015
Amago vassal. After the Amago fell he served the Toyotomi as lord of Shikano Castle, Inaba. Promoted land reclamation, canals and industry; also sent licensed trading ships to Siam.

@23016
Akamatsu Yoshisuke ?-1576

@23017
Lord of Shinjoyama Castle, Mimasaka. Allied with Oda Nobunaga to revive the fallen Akamatsu, but was beaten by Uragami Masamune and lost still more power.

@23018
Asano Yoshinaga 1576-1613

@23019
Heir of Nagamasa. Sided with the East at Sekigahara and received Wakayama, Kii (370,000 koku). With Kato Kiyomasa he arranged the meeting of Tokugawa Ieyasu and Toyotomi Hideyori.

@23020
Nanbu Toshinao 1576-1632

@23021
Son of Nobunao; succeeded on his father's death. Sided with the East at Sekigahara and relieved the Mogami when the Uesugi attacked them. Crushed uprisings at home, showing the Nanbu's might.

@23022
Kunohe Sanechika ?-1591

@23023
Younger brother of Masazane. Married Nanbu Harumasa's daughter and was a candidate for heir. Rebelled against the Nanbu with Masazane, fought the Toyotomi army under Gamo Ujisato and others, and fell.

@23024
Hachinohe Masahide 1543-1610

@23025
With Kita Nobuchika, a pillar of the early Nanbu domain. Trusted by his lord Nobunao, he kept Sannohe Castle while Nobunao joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara.

@23026
Osaki Yoshinao ?-1577

@23027
12th head of the Osaki. When his vassal Niida Yorito and others revolted, he crushed them with Date Tanemune's help. Thereafter submitted to the Date to keep his lands.

@23028
Tsugaru Nobuhira 1586-1631

@23029
Third son of Tamenobu; baptised a Christian as a child at his father's urging. Succeeded as 2nd lord of the Hirosaki domain in Oshu; built Hirosaki Castle and secured the domain.

@23030
Numata Sukemitsu ?-1612

@23031
Tsugaru vassal from Numata in Kozuke. Travelling the land to train as a warrior, he heard of Tamenobu's character and became his strategist; did great service in Tamenobu's unification of Tsugaru.

@23032
Morioka Nobumoto ?-1600

@23033
Tsugaru vassal, one of the three Oura elders who aided Tsugaru Tamenobu's rise. Took and held Watoku Castle in the war with Nanbu Takanobu. Later suspected of treason by Tamenobu and murdered.

@23034
Kasai Chikanobu 1513-1560

@23035
Heir of Harutane; became 16th head of the Kasai. Sickly, he died after only 5 years of rule; his younger brother Harunobu succeeded him.

@23036
Kashiyama Akisuke 1578-1624

@23037
Kasai vassal, highest in rank among them. After the Kasai fell he served the Nanbu, but his lord Toshinao, fearing his prowess, invited him to Hanamaki Castle and poisoned him.

@23038
Nikaido Moriyoshi ?-1581

@23039
18th head of the Nikaido. Fought Ashina Moriuji but surrendered, sending his son Moritaka as hostage. When Moritaka later became head of the Ashina, he used his aid to restore the Nikaido.

@23040
Osaki Yoshinobu 1526-1550

@23041
11th head of the Osaki; second son of Date Tanemune. Married Osaki Takakane's daughter and succeeded. Lost in the Date civil war (Tenbun Rebellion); slain while fleeing to Kasai lands.

@23042
Kurokawa Haruuji 1523-1599

@23043
Osaki kinsman who followed the Date. When Date Masamune invaded Osaki lands, ostensibly to aid the Ujiie faction, he betrayed the Date, backed the main faction and beat the Date army.

@23044
Nanjo Takanobu ?-?

@23045
Osaki vassal in Oshu; a Samurai General. As keeper of Nakanishinden Castle he crushed the Date army in the Osaki war and was hailed as a great general; his tactics lived long in war history.

@23046
Inawashiro Morikuni 1536-?

@23047
Ashina vassal. Having handed over the headship, he was swayed by his second wife's slander, tried to disinherit Moritane and fought him. Later left the Ashina and led the Date van at Suriagehara.

@23048
Inawashiro Moritane ?-1589

@23049
Ashina vassal. At Suriagehara, learning that his father Morikuni had gone over to the Date, he drew back once, then charged the Date army again and was badly wounded in the melee.

@23050
Kanagami Moriharu 1527-1589

@23051
Ashina vassal. After Moritaka died, he checked the faction backing Kojiro, Date Masamune's brother, and brought in Yoshihiro, second son of Satake Yoshishige, as head. Fell at Suriagehara.

@23052
Nihonmatsu Yoshikuni ?-1580

@23053
14th head of the Nihonmatsu Hatakeyama of Oshu. His father Shinjo Muranao was a brother of the 10th head Murakuni. When the 13th head Yoshiuji left no heir, he joined the Nihonmatsu and succeeded.

@23054
Ouchi Sadatsuna 1545-1610

@23055
Lord of Shiomatsu Castle, Oshu; a fine spearman. Submitted when Masamune became head of the Date, but turned on him the next year. Beaten by Masamune, he served the Date from then on.

@23056
Soma Akitane 1508-1549

@23057
14th head of the Soma; son of Moritane, lord of Odaka Castle in Oshu. In the Date civil war (Tenbun Rebellion) fought hard for his father-in-law Date Tanemune and sheltered him when Harumune pressed him.

@23058
Iwaki Chikataka ?-1594

@23059
Eldest son of Date Harumune. Adopted by Iwaki Shigetaka, he succeeded to the domain his foster father had built. Later submitted to Satake Yoshishige, sworn enemy of the Date.

@23060
Iwaki Tsunetaka 1567-1590

@23061
Son of Chikataka; succeeded his father as lord of Odate Castle, Mutsu. Joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara and kept his lands, but died at Kamakura soon after the Hojo surrendered.

@23062
Iwaki Sadataka 1583-1620

@23063
Third son of Satake Yoshishige; adopted heir of Iwaki Tsunetaka. Attainted for not taking the field at Sekigahara. Fought at Osaka under Honda Masanobu; restored with 10,000 koku at Kawanakajima, Shinano.

@23064
Tamura Takaaki ?-1574

@23065
Lord of Miharu Castle, Oshu. Fought on Tanemune's side in the Date civil war (Tenbun Rebellion). Later repelled the joint invasion of Satake Yoshishige and Ishikawa Akimitsu.

@23066
Tamura Kiyoaki ?-1586

@23067
Son of Takaaki; lord of Miharu Castle, Oshu. His daughter Megohime married Date Masamune. Succeeded his father Takaaki and led the Tamura on the attack until his death.

@23068
Ujiie Mitsuuji ?-?

@23069
Mogami vassal from the Narisawa house. At Sekigahara he fought hard against Uesugi Kagekatsu's invading army. When Iechika became head, he took charge of governing the domain.

@23070
Komine Yoshichika 1541-1626

@23071
Lord of Komine Castle, Oshu, from the Komine branch of the Shirakawa Yuki. Drove out Yoshiaki of the main line and seized the house. Beaten by Satake Yoshishige, he adopted his son Yoshihiro and retired.

@23072
Ando Kiyosue ?-1554

@23073
7th head of the Hiyama Ando. In Ezo he arbitrated a peace between Kakizaki Suehiro and the Ainu, strengthening the Hiyama Ando's hold on Ezo.

@23074
Ando Shigesue ?-1579

@23075
Son of Kiyosue; younger brother of Tomosue and Chikasue. When Tomosue, Takasue's adopted heir, died, he married Kakizaki Suehiro's sixth daughter and took his brother's place as head of the Minato Ando.

@23076
Ando Michisue ?-?

@23077
Heir of Shigesue. After his father's death his uncle Chikasue drove him from Minato Castle to Toshima Castle. Later rebelled, but was beaten by Sanesue, Chikasue's son.

@23078
Ando Takasue ?-1551

@23079
Head of the Ando; son of Nobusue. Fittingly for a clan of sea raiders he styled himself "Lay Monk Iron Ship Hermit". Having no son, he adopted the son of Kiyosue, his son-in-law, as heir.

@23080
Asari Noriyori ?-1550

@23081
Hinai landholder in Dewa; son of Asayori. Moved from Kai to Dewa, built Jukkonai Castle and established his rule. Often clashed and fought with the neighbouring Ando.

@23082
Asari Norisuke ?-1562

@23083
Hinai landholder in Dewa. Sought independence for his domain, but his brother Katsuyori schemed to submit to the Ando. Attacked by an Ando army guided in by his brother, he killed himself.

@23084
Asari Katsuyori ?-1583

@23085
Younger brother of Norisuke. Conspired with the Ando to force his brother's suicide and became head. Later plotted rebellion with Tsugaru Tamenobu; Ando Chikasue lured him to a banquet and killed him.

@23086
Onodera Terumichi ?-1597

@23087
13th head of the Onodera. Holding that his father Norimichi was murdered, not a suicide, he slew the foe Kanazawa Kanjobo and became lord of Yokote Castle, Dewa. Led the Onodera to their peak.

@23088
Rokugo Masanori 1567-1634

@23089
Dewa landholder. Submitted to Toyotomi Hideyoshi and guarded Nagoya Castle during the Korean campaign. Sided with the East at Sekigahara, fought at Osaka too, and became lord of Honjo domain.

@23090
Sakenobe Hidetsuna 1562-1646

@23091
Lord of Sakenobe Castle, Dewa. A guest general of the Onodera, he was beaten by Mogami Yoshiaki and served him; won merit quelling a Senboku uprising and fighting the Uesugi in Mogami lands.

@23092
Tozawa Michimori 1524-1604

@23093
16th head of the Tozawa; lord of Kakunodate Castle, Dewa. Succeeded aged only 6 on his father Hidemori's death. Retired while still young and lived out his days.

@23094
Satomi Yoshiyasu 1573-1603

@23095
Son of Yoshiyori; built Tateyama Castle in Awa. Late for Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara, he lost Kazusa and kept only Awa, but the Satomi clan survived.

@23096
Tozawa Masamori 1585-1648

@23097
Son of Moriyasu. At 8 he succeeded his uncle Mitsumori as 20th head of the Tozawa. Took the Uesugi's Sakata Castle, Dewa, at Sekigahara; guarded Odawara and Edo Castles during Osaka.

@23098
Mogami Iechika 1582-1617

@23099
Second son of Yoshiaki; fought at Ueda Castle, Shinano, in the Sekigahara war. On his father's death he destroyed his brother Yoshichika and enlarged his domain, but died young; some say his uncle Tateoka Mitsunao killed him.
