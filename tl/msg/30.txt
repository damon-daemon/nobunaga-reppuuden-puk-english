Arima Toyoji 1569-1642

@30001
Noriyori's heir. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi, then Tokugawa Ieyasu. Distinguished himself at Sekigahara and Osaka; held Kurume in Chikugo, 210,000 koku. Also fought in the Shimabara Rebellion.

@30002
Miyoshi Masanaga ?-1549

@30003
A vassal of the Hosokawa Kanrei; lord of Enami Castle in Settsu and a close aide of Harumoto. Beat Miyoshi Chokei when he rose in arms, but later lost at Eguchi in Settsu and died in battle.

@30004
Iwanari Tomomichi ?-1575

@30005
One of the Miyoshi Triumvirate, treated as one of the Miyoshi kin. Submitted to Oda Nobunaga, but answered the Shogun Ashikaga Yoshiaki's call to arms and held Yodo Castle in Yamashiro. Died fighting Nobunaga's army.

@30006
Matsunaga Hisamichi ?-1577

@30007
Hisahide's heir; lord of Tamonyama Castle in Yamato. Murdered the Shogun Ashikaga Yoshiteru and later submitted to Oda Nobunaga. In the end he rebelled with his father at Shigisan Castle in Yamato and killed himself.

@30008
Hatakeyama Masakuni ?-1550

@30009
Head of the Kawachi Hatakeyama; succeeded his brother Tanenaga. Based at Takaya Castle in Kawachi, he fought Miyoshi Yoshikata and others as Constable of Kawachi. Died at Iwamuro Castle in Kii.

@30010
Yusa Naganori ?-1551

@30011
A vassal of the Kawachi Hatakeyama and Deputy Constable of Kawachi; his daughter married Miyoshi Chokei. Trusted by Hatakeyama Akitaka, he betrayed him, forced him to kill himself and took power. Later murdered.

@30012
Yusa Nobunori ?-1572

@30013
A Hatakeyama vassal and Deputy Constable of Kawachi. Banished his lord Takamasa to Kii and made Takamasa's brother Akitaka head. Later killed Akitaka too, but was struck down by Oda Nobunaga.

@30014
Tsutsui Junsho 1523-1550

@30015
Head of the Tsutsui, a local house of Yamato. Sided with the Kizawa in their feud with the Kawachi Hatakeyama. When the Kizawa lost he promptly changed sides, and with Hatakeyama help expanded his power.

@30016
Matsukura Shigenobu ?-1593

@30017
A Tsutsui vassal; held 2,500 koku under Junkei. After Junkei's death, when his adopted son Sadatsugu was moved to Ueno in Iga, he built Nabari Castle in Iga and held 8,300 koku.

@30018
Matsukura Shigemasa ?-1630

@30019
Shigenobu's son. After the Tsutsui's fall he served Tokugawa Ieyasu; after Osaka, lord of Shimabara Castle in Hizen. His harsh rule, heavy taxes and persecution of Christians caused the Shimabara Rebellion.

@30020
Hashio Takaharu ?-?

@30021
A Tsutsui vassal, trusted by Junkei, whose sister he married. Did not follow the Tsutsui to Iga but served Toyotomi Hideyoshi in Yamato. Lost his lands after Sekigahara and became a ronin.

@30022
Araki Mataemon 1599-1638

@30023
Real name Yasutomo; fencing instructor of Koriyama domain in Yamato. His help in his brother-in-law's revenge later became the "36 men cut down" scene of the tale "Kagiya no Tsuji".

@30024
Tsuchibashi Morishige ?-1582

@30025
A local samurai leading the Saika band of Kii. When Oda Nobunaga attacked the Ishiyama Honganji, he allied with the Ikko, raised a relief force and fought Nobunaga. Later submitted, but was killed by Suzuki Shigetomo.

@30026
Isshiki Yoshiyuki ?-?

@30027
Head of the Isshiki. Fought his ancestral foe, the Wakasa Takeda, for control of Tango. Without any great success, he handed the house to his son Yoshimichi and retired.

@30028
Isshiki Yoshisada ?-1582

@30029
Yoshimichi's son. After his father killed himself when his vassals betrayed him, he held Yumiki Castle in Tango against Hosokawa Fujitaka. Akechi Mitsuhide arranged peace, but he defied Fujitaka and was murdered.

@30030
Isshiki Yoshikiyo ?-1582

@30031
An Isshiki vassal. When Hosokawa Fujitaka murdered his nephew Yoshisada, he took over the Isshiki at Yumiki Castle in Tango. Besieged by Hosokawa Tadaoki, he charged into the enemy and died.

@30032
Inatomi Sukehide ?-?

@30033
An Isshiki vassal; lord of Shinoki Castle in Tango and Sukenao's grandfather. Served Yoshisada. Invited the gunner Sasaki Yoshikuni and studied gunnery, later teaching it to his grandson Sukenao.

@30034
Kakiya Tsugunari 1482-1570

@30035
A Yamana vassal. When Yamana Okitoyo became Constable of Tajima, he became deputy and ran the province. Later fell out with Tainosho Koreyoshi, a Tajima local lord; attacked by surprise, he killed himself.

@30036
Kakiya Mitsunari ?-1592

@30037
Tsugunari's son. Served the Yamana, but surrendered when attacked by Hashiba Hideyoshi. Fought in the main force against Tottori Castle in Inaba and was given 10,000 koku in Inaba.

@30038
Kakiya Tsunefusa ?-1600

@30039
Said to be Mitsunari's son. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi at Odawara and in the Korean campaign. Joined the West at Sekigahara and attacked Otsu Castle in Omi. After the war he killed himself on Mt. Koya.

@30040
Tainosho Koreyoshi ?-1575

@30041
One of the Four Heavenly Kings of the Yamana; lord of Tsuru Castle in Tajima. Urged submission to the Oda, against the Kakiya, who leant to the Mori. Killed Kakiya Tsugunari, but was slain by his son Mitsunari.

@30042
Yoshioka Sadakatsu 1532-1606

@30043
A Yamana vassal. Held Tsuzurao Castle in Inaba against Hashiba Hideyoshi's great army. Defended skilfully with the Mori and troubled Hideyoshi, but starved out by siege, he lost his men and was beaten.

@30044
Nanjo Munekatsu 1498-1575

@30045
7th head of the Nanjo; lord of Ueshi Castle. Lost his castle to the Amago invasion and became a ronin. Later took service with the Yamana and won back Ueshi Castle.

@30046
Nanjo Mototsugu ?-1591

@30047
Lord of Ueshi Castle in Hoki; succeeded on his father Munekatsu's death. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi in the Kyushu campaign. Ill, he left the government to his brother Motokiyo, but still went to Odawara.

@30048
Nanjo Mototada ?-1614

@30049
Mototsugu's son; succeeded on his father's death. Stripped of his domain for siding with the West at Sekigahara. Later served Toyotomi Hideyori, but was killed for secretly dealing with the Tokugawa in the winter siege of Osaka.

@30050
Amago Yoshihisa 1540-1610

@30051
Haruhisa's heir. Held Gassan-Toda Castle in Izumo against Mori Motonari, but undone by Motonari's divisive schemes, he surrendered. Confined in Aki with his brothers.

@30052
Uyama Hisakane ?-1566

@30053
Chief elder of the Amago. In the siege of Gassan-Toda Castle in Izumo he bought food with his own money for the garrison. Later killed by his lord Yoshihisa, who fell for Mori Motonari's divisive scheme.

@30054
Sase Kiyomune ?-?

@30055
An Amago vassal; an elder since Haruhisa's time, he joined the attack on the Mori at Yoshida-Koriyama. Fought hard in the siege of Gassan-Toda, but submitted to the Mori just before it fell.

@30056
Sase Motoyoshi 1546-1620

@30057
An Amago vassal. Surrendered with his father Kiyomune to Mori Motonari's invasion of Izumo. Then served the Mori in government; kept Hiroshima Castle during the Korean campaign and Sekigahara.

@30058
Akana Morikiyo 1529-1595

@30059
An Amago vassal. Gained land for his father's merits, but when the feud with the Mori grew fierce he left the fast-declining Amago and joined Mori Motonari.

@30060
Honjo Tsunemitsu ?-1562

@30061
An Amago vassal. Briefly followed the Ouchi after a defeat, then returned to the Amago. Holding Yamabuki Castle by the Iwami Silver Mine, he troubled Mori Motonari, but submitted for more land.

@30062
Yoshimi Hiroyori 1535-1613

@30063
Masayori's son. His wife died before him, and his heir Motoyori was killed in the Korean campaign. He lost his second wife and his daughters too, and spent his last years alone.

@30064
Masuda Fujikane 1529-1596

@30065
Lord of Masuda Castle in Iwami. Served the Ouchi; after their fall he followed the Mori and kept his lands. Among his men was Shinagawa Daizen, who fought a duel with Yamanaka Shikanosuke.

@30066
Masuda Motonaga 1558-1640

@30067
Fujikane's son. Served the Mori, with merit in the conquest of Chugoku. When the Mori's lands were cut after Sekigahara, he took charge of rebuilding their finances and did so brilliantly.

@30068
Bessho Nariharu 1502-1563

@30069
Lord of Miki Castle in Harima. Allied with the Uragami against the Akamatsu. Lost his seat to the Uragami but soon returned, and expanded as the Akamatsu declined. Later joined Miyoshi Chokei.

@30070
Bessho Yasuharu 1532-1570

@30071
A Miyoshi vassal; lord of Miki Castle in Harima, son of Nariharu. Submitted to Oda Nobunaga, master of the capital region, and steadily expanded in east Harima. The house peaked under his son Nagaharu.

@30072
Bessho Shigemune ?-1591

@30073
Nariharu's son. After his brother Yasuharu died, he aided his nephew Nagaharu and served Oda Nobunaga. A ronin after Nagaharu's death; served Toyotomi Hideyoshi for 15,000 koku and went on the Kyushu campaign.

@30074
Bessho Yoshiharu ?-?

@30075
Shigemune's son; held Sonobe in Tanba, 15,000 koku. Joined the West at Sekigahara and attacked Tanabe Castle in Tango. Kept his lands, but was later stripped of them for neglect of duty.

@30076
Uragami Masamune ?-1566

@30077
An Akamatsu vassal, more powerful than his lords. Married his son Kiyomune to Kuroda Mototaka's daughter to ally with Mototaka's lord Odera Masamoto. Killed by Akamatsu Masahide on the wedding day.

@30078
Odera Masamoto ?-?

@30079
Lord of Gochaku Castle in Harima; Kuroda Kanbei's first lord. Allied with the Harima clans against Oda Nobunaga's western campaign, but was beaten. What became of him after is unknown.

@30080
Akamatsu Harumasa 1513-?

@30081
Inherited from his father Yoshimura, but was held as a puppet by Uragami Muramune. Later struck down Muramune with Hosokawa Harumoto's help, but attacked anew by the Amago, he could not restore his house.

@30082
Akamatsu Norifusa ?-1598

@30083
Yoshisuke's son. Joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi and kept Oshio in Harima, 10,000 koku. Served at Shizugatake, Komaki-Nagakute and in the Shikoku campaign, then received 10,000 koku more at Sumiyoshi in Awa.

@30084
Akamatsu Masahide ?-1570

@30085
Lord of Tatsuno Castle in Harima. Killed the arrogant Uragami Masamune; allied with Bessho Yasuharu, fought Odera Masamoto and Kuroda Mototaka. Strove to restore the Akamatsu, but died suddenly; poisoned, it is said.

@30086
Saimura Masahiro 1562-1600

@30087
Akamatsu Masahide's son, versed in Chinese learning. At Sekigahara he went from the West to the East and joined the attack on Tottori Castle. For burning the castle town, Tokugawa Ieyasu ordered him to kill himself.

@30088
Nakayama Nobumasa ?-1559

@30089
An Uragami vassal; lord of Kameyama Castle in Bizen. His daughter married Ukita Naoie. Suspected of planning revolt, he was murdered by Ukita Naoie on Uragami Munekage's orders.

@30090
Shimamura Kanami ?-1559

@30091
An Uragami vassal of the Shimamura, who shared power in the house with the Ukita. Murdered his rival Ukita Yoshiie at Toishi Castle and seized power alone. Later killed by Yoshiie's grandson Naoie.

@30092
Oka Toshikatsu ?-1592

@30093
An Ukita vassal; one of the "Three Ukita Elders". A warrior skilled with sword and spear who fought in over 40 battles. Rebuilt Okayama Castle in Bizen and its town. Died of illness on campaign in Korea.

@30094
Osafune Sadachika ?-1588

@30095
An Ukita vassal; one of the "Three Ukita Elders". Fought in many places, helping his lord Naoie build the house. Ran the government after Togawa Hideyasu retired. Later killed by his sister's husband.

@30096
Osafune Tsunanao ?-1598

@30097
Sadachika's son. Governed with Toyotomi Hideyoshi's backing, surveying the land and building up the castle town. Favoured by his lord Hideie, he wielded great power; said to have been poisoned by rival vassals.

@30098
Togawa Michiyasu 1567-1627

@30099
Hideyasu's heir. Served the Ukita, distinguishing himself in the Korean campaign. A Nichiren believer, he clashed with the Christian Osafune Tsunanao. Fell from Hideie's favour and fled.
