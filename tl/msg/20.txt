Konchiin Suden 1569-1633

@20001
Tokugawa vassal who drafted laws such as the Code for the Warrior Houses. Stirred up the Hokoji bell affair, giving the pretext for the Osaka campaigns. As "the Black-Robed Minister" he ruled the Tokugawa from the shadows.

@20002
Oniniwa Tsunamoto 1549-1640

@20003
Date Masamune's confidant. In the Korean campaign received Toyotomi Hideyoshi's concubine Lady Ko, angering Masamune, and fled. Later forgiven and returned, helping govern as a senior elder.

@20004
Kasai Harunobu 1534-1597

@20005
Harutane's second son; succeeded his brother Chikanobu as lord of Teraike Castle in Mutsu. Allied with the Date against the Osaki. Lost his lands for failing to join Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara.

@20006
Nanbu Harumasa 1517-1582

@20007
24th head of the Nanbu. Given a character of the Shogun Ashikaga Yoshiharu's name, took the name Harumasa. From Sannohe Castle in Oshu built a vast domain, said to stretch "as far as the crescent moon rounds".

@20008
Narita Ujinaga 1542-1595

@20009
Nagayasu's son; a Hojo vassal. In Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara held out in Odawara Castle. His own Oshi Castle, flooded by Ishida Mitsunari, did not fall until after Odawara surrendered.

@20010
Kunohe Masazane 1536-1591

@20011
Nanbu vassal. After the head Harumasa's death, backed his brother Sanechika as heir; when he lost, revolted. Fought well against the great Toyotomi army called in by Nanbu Nobunao, but lost and was beheaded.

@20012
Matsumae Yoshihiro 1549-1616

@20013
Son of Kakizaki Suehiro. Drew close to Toyotomi Hideyoshi, won rule over Ezo and broke free of the Ando. The name Matsumae takes one character each from Matsudaira (Ieyasu) and Maeda (Toshiie).

@20014
Kanamori Nagachika 1524-1608

@20015
Oda vassal; one of the Red Cloaks. After Nobunaga served Toyotomi Hideyoshi and held 38,000 koku at Takayama in Hida. Learned tea from Sen no Rikyu and is counted among his Seven Disciples.

@20016
Onodera Yoshimichi 1566-1645

@20017
Kagemichi's son. Lost much land in defeat after defeat, then, tricked by Mogami Yoshiaki, killed senior retainers and drove off his vassals. Lost his lands for turning to the West at Sekigahara.

@20018
Shimura Mitsuyasu ?-1609

@20019
Mogami vassal, trusted by all: "even the strongest foe yielded to him". With Ujiie Morimune served as Yoshiaki's confidant, and at Sekigahara repelled Naoe Kanetsugu's army.

@20020
Ujiie Morimune ?-?

@20021
Sadanao's son; like his father, served the Mogami. A clever schemer, used many ruses to destroy the Tendo and Shiratori. Won glory in many places, such as the conquest of the Mamurogawa area.

@20022
Hachisuka Iemasa 1559-1638

@20023
Masakatsu's heir. Served Toyotomi Hideyoshi and with his father held 180,000 koku at Tokushima in Awa. Sided with the West at Sekigahara, but kept his lands because his son Yoshishige joined the East.

@20024
Ashina Morishige 1576-1631

@20025
Satake Yoshishige's second son. Adopted by the Shirakawa-Yuki, then married Ashina Moritaka's daughter and became 20th head of the Ashina. Lost to Date Masamune at Suriagehara and fled to his native Hitachi.

@20026
Saionji Kinhiro ?-1587

@20027
Lord of Kurose Castle in Iyo. Left the priesthood to succeed his cousin Kintaka, killed in battle. Became a vassal of Chosokabe Motochika after defeat; later yielded to Toyotomi Hideyoshi; murdered by Toda Katsutaka.

@20028
Tomita Takazane 1569-?

@20029
Ashina vassal. At Suriagehara beat Inawashiro Morikuni and Katakura Kagetsuna and closed on the Date headquarters, but was overwhelmed by numbers. Escorted his lord Morishige to Hitachi.

@20030
Satake Yoshiaki 1531-1565

@20031
17th head of the Satake. Subdued the Shirakawa-Yuki of southern Mutsu and fought the Oda for Hitachi. Refused shelter to Kanto Kanrei Uesugi Norimasa when, pressed by the Hojo, he asked for it.

@20032
Kamiizumi Nobutsuna 1508-1573

@20033
Once a local lord in Kozuke, he served Nagano Narimasa and Narimori. After they fell, wandered the land to master the sword. Founded Shinkage-ryu and was called a sword saint.

@20034
Utsunomiya Hirotsuna 1543-1580

@20035
22nd head of the Utsunomiya. With help from the Satake, his wife's family, subdued the anti-Utsunomiya forces in Shimotsuke. Allied with the Uesugi of Echigo against the Hojo push north.

@20036
Yuki Masakatsu 1503-1559

@20037
16th head of the Yuki. Laid down the "New Yuki Code" to tighten his rule. Backed by Hojo Ujiyasu, then spreading into northern Kanto, fought the Satake, Utsunomiya, Oda and others.

@20038
Satomi Yoshihiro 1530-1578

@20039
Yoshitaka's eldest son. Allied with Ota Sukemasa and others against Hojo Ujiyasu at Konodai, but lost. Later won at Mifuneyama, and after his father's death made peace with the Hojo.

@20040
Mishuku Kanbee ?-1615

@20041
Real name Masatomo; a former Hojo retainer. After the Imagawa and Takeda served Yuki Hideyasu, but left under Hideyasu's son Tadanao and joined the Toyotomi. Killed in the Osaka Summer Campaign.

@20042
Daihoji Yoshiuji 1551-1583

@20043
Lord of Oura Castle in Dewa. Always at war and neglecting his domain, he was hated by his people as "the Wicked Lord". Overthrown by retainers in league with Mogami Yoshiaki, killed himself.

@20044
Hojo Ujikuni 1541-1597

@20045
Ujiyasu's third son; lord of Hachigata Castle in Musashi. Concluded the alliance with the Uesugi. In Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara urged a sortie. Attacked by Maeda Toshiie's army, was beaten and surrendered.

@20046
Hojo Ujinori 1545-1600

@20047
Ujiyasu's fourth son. A hostage of the Imagawa, he befriended Tokugawa Ieyasu, also a hostage there. In Toyotomi Hideyoshi's siege of Odawara held Nirayama Castle in Izu, but yielded at Ieyasu's urging.

@20048
Daidoji Masashige 1533-1590

@20049
Served three Hojo heads: Ujiyasu, Ujimasa and Ujinao. In the siege of Odawara held Matsuida Castle in Kozuke, lasting a month while the other castles of Kozuke fell. Made to kill himself after the war.

@20050
Matsuda Norihide ?-1590

@20051
Hojo vassal and chief elder. When war with the Toyotomi was certain, urged holding out in the castle. Later tried to defect to Hideyoshi, but failed, and was made to kill himself by Hideyoshi after the war.

@20052
Itabeoka Kosessai 1537-1609

@20053
Hojo monk-diplomat. Showed his skill in peace talks with Tokugawa Ieyasu and dealings with Toyotomi Hideyoshi. After the Hojo fell, became one of Hideyoshi's storytellers, then served Ieyasu.

@20054
Oniniwa Yoshinao 1513-1585

@20055
Date vassal. Spotted for his talent by Terumune, rose to senior retainer. Given overall command at Hitotoribashi, he lost, and though old led the rearguard and was killed.

@20056
Nishina Morinobu 1557-1582

@20057
Takeda Shingen's fifth son. Trusted by his brother Katsuyori, held the key castle of Takato in Shinano. Bravely withstood the Oda army in a siege, but was overwhelmed by numbers and killed himself in the castle.

@20058
Anayama Nobukimi 1541-1582

@20059
Takeda vassal; foremost of the Takeda kin. In battle usually guarded the headquarters. When the Takeda fell, surrendered to Tokugawa Ieyasu. Hearing of Honnoji, headed home from Sakai and was killed by peasants.

@20060
Hara Toratane 1497-1564

@20061
Takeda vassal; a hero wounded 53 times in 38 battles, called "Yaksha Mino". Briefly served the Hojo, but came back after only a year.

@20062
Oyamada Nobushige 1539-1582

@20063
Takeda vassal who led a stone-throwing unit in many battles. When Oda Nobunaga invaded Kai, abandoned Katsuyori and surrendered, but was cut down as a traitor.

@20064
Akiyama Nobutomo 1531-1575

@20065
Takeda vassal; led the Ina men. A fierce warrior whom Tokugawa Ieyasu called "the Takeda's raging bull". Held Iwamura Castle in Mino against Oda Nobunaga, but lost, was captured and executed.

@20066
Obu Toramasa 1504-1565

@20067
Takeda vassal, also called "the Fierce Tiger of Kai". Led an elite unit clad all in red. Tutor to Shingen's eldest son Yoshinobu, he was held responsible for Yoshinobu's plot and killed himself.

@20068
Kiso Yoshimasa 1540-1595

@20069
Yoshiyasu's son; married a daughter of Takeda Shingen. Under Katsuyori secretly sided with Oda Nobunaga and surrendered to him; later joined Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Lost the Kiso valley and was moved to Shimosa.

@20070
Sanada Nobutsuna 1537-1575

@20071
Yukitaka's eldest son. After his father retired, fought on many fronts as a Shinshu vanguard captain, winning renown. At Nagashino fought with a huge sword over three feet long, but fell in a hail of bullets.

@20072
Nasu Sukeharu 1557-1610

@20073
Descendant of Nasu no Yoichi, the master archer who served Minamoto no Yoritomo. Routed Utsunomiya Hirotsuna at Mt. Kumashita, winning fame. Lost his lands for arriving late to the siege of Odawara.

@20074
Kikkawa Tsuneie 1547-1581

@20075
Mori vassal. Held Tottori Castle in Inaba against Hashiba Hideyoshi of the Oda. Starved out in the siege called "death by hunger", killed himself in exchange for his garrison's lives.

@20076
Uesugi Kagetora 1554-1579

@20077
Hojo Ujiyasu's seventh son. Sent as a hostage when the Hojo and Uesugi allied, was adopted by Kenshin. After Kenshin's death fought Uesugi Kagekatsu for the succession, lost and killed himself.

@20078
Nagao Masakage 1526-1564

@20079
Father of Uesugi Kagekatsu. Rebelled just after Kenshin became head, but lost and made peace. A pillar of the Uesugi, he was left in charge of Kasugayama Castle in Echigo and more.

@20080
Naoe Kagetsuna 1509-1577

@20081
Uesugi vassal. Kenshin's trusted aide, a sharp hand in government and diplomacy. Given the character "Kage" from Kenshin's old name Kagetora, he changed his name from Sanetsuna to Kagetsuna.

@20082
Usami Sadamitsu 1489-1564

@20083
Uesugi vassal; founder of the Echigo school of war and Kenshin's strategist. Made peace between Kenshin and Nagao Masakage. Said to have died fighting the Hojo, or drowned boating with Masakage.

@20084
Kakizaki Kageie ?-1575

@20085
Uesugi vassal; the clan's fiercest warrior, of whom Kenshin said "none in Echigo's seven districts can match him". Led the Uesugi main force, but was killed by Kenshin on suspicion of dealings with Oda Nobunaga.

@20086
Saito Tomonobu ?-1591

@20087
Uesugi vassal. Shone at Kawanakajima, Sano Castle in Shimotsuke and elsewhere, called "the Shoki of Echigo". Served Kagekatsu after Kenshin's death. Unselfish and also a skilled administrator.

@20088
Jojo Masashige 1553-1625

@20089
Hatakeyama Yoshitsugu's second son. Adopted by Uesugi Kenshin, became head of the Jojo. Fought in Kozuke, Etchu and elsewhere. After Kenshin's death quarrelled with Uesugi Kagekatsu over Shinshu lands and left.

@20090
Hojo Genan 1493-1589

@20091
Soun's third son. Served five generations of Hojo from Soun to Ujinao; famed as a man of letters. Said to have given his "Genan Memoranda", a book of advice, to Ujiyasu's daughter on her marriage.

@20092
Honjo Shigenaga 1539-1613

@20093
Uesugi vassal. Won glory at Kawanakajima and in the Kanto campaigns. Attacked and beaten by Kenshin for secret dealings with the Takeda; became a ronin, but later returned to service.

@20094
Sagara Taketo 1498-1551

@20095
Ouchi vassal. Trusted by his lord Yoshitaka, led the civil faction against Sue Harukata, head of the military faction, provoking Harukata's revolt. Beaten by Sue's army in Chikuzen, killed himself.

@20096
Ashina Morioki 1547-1575

@20097
Moriuji's heir; became 17th head of the Ashina when his father retired, but died young while at war with the Satake. His father once banned brewing sake in his lands to cure his illness.

@20098
Jinbo Nagamoto ?-?

@20099
Lord of Toyama Castle in Etchu. Attacked and beaten by Uesugi Kenshin, abandoned his castle and fled to Masuyama Castle in Etchu. Later submitted to Kenshin and held Masuyama against the Ikko rebels.
