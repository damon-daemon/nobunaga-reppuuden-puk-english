@24000
Yamanobe Yoshitada 1588-1664

@24001
Fourth son of Mogami Yoshiaki. Put forward as head in place of his nephew Yoshitoshi, but many objected; the strife that followed got the Mogami attainted. Later an elder of the Mito Tokugawa.

@24002
Niizeki Hisamasa ?-?

@24003
Hereditary Mogami vassal styled Governor of Inaba. Skilled in farming, he was put in charge of irrigation and flood control on the Shonai plain; his weir is still called the "Inaba Weir".

@24004
Ujiie Sadanao ?-?

@24005
Mogami vassal. Took the field for his lord Yoshimori in the Date civil war (Tenbun Rebellion). In the Mogami succession dispute he mediated from his sickbed.

@24006
Shimizu Yoshiuji ?-1586

@24007
Lord of Shimizu Castle, Dewa. Having no son, he adopted Yoshichika, third son of Mogami Yoshiaki. Twice repelled raids from Shonai with an army joined with Yoshiaki's.

@24008
Shimizu Yoshichika 1582-1614

@24009
Third son of Yoshiaki; lord of Shimizu Castle. Half-brother of Iechika, born the same year. Adopted by the sonless Shimizu Yoshiuji. Later clashed with his brother Iechika; attacked, he took his life.

@24010
Nobesawa Mitsunobu 1544-1591

@24011
Mogami vassal of great strength. When Yoshiaki playfully clung to a cherry tree, he pulled to drag him off, and the trunk, some 60 cm round, snapped and fell.

@24012
Tateoka Mitsushige ?-?

@24013
Mogami vassal; lord of Yuzawa Castle, Dewa. Attacked by the Onodera during the Sekigahara war, he held the castle alone. His 48,000 koku was the largest fief among the Mogami vassals.

@24014
Daihoji Yoshimasu ?-?

@24015
Head of the Daihoji; father of Yoshiuji. The house began when Ujihira, son of Muto Yorihira of the Fujiwara, became steward of Oizumi estate in early Kamakura times; it took the Daihoji name under the Muromachi.

@24016
Daihoji Yoshioki ?-1587

@24017
Lord of Ora Castle, Dewa; succeeded his brother Yoshiuji. Adopted the second son of Uesugi vassal Honjo Shigenaga to bind himself to the Uesugi, but Mogami Yoshiaki attacked and he took his life.

@24018
Daihoji Yoshikatsu 1573-1623

@24019
Second son of Honjo Shigenaga. Adopted by Daihoji Yoshioki, he took over the Daihoji with Uesugi Kagekatsu's backing. Exiled to Yamato on suspicion of stirring revolt; pardoned, he became an Uesugi vassal.

@24020
Hojo Ujishige 1536-1578

@24021
Son of Tsunashige. Every bit as brave as his father, called the fiercest of the Hojo generals. Held Tamanawa Castle, Sagami, and won fame repelling the invading army of Uesugi Kenshin.

@24022
Date Tanemune 1488-1565

@24023
14th head of the Date; great-grandfather of Masamune. Won the post of Constable of Oshu and steadily grew his domain through political marriages. His clash with his son Harumune caused the Tenbun Rebellion.

@24024
Rusu Masakage 1549-1607

@24025
Third son of Date Harumune. Married the daughter of Rusu Akimune and took over the Rusu. Served his brother Terumune and nephew Masamune, fighting the Soma and Mogami. Granted the Date name after Korea.

@24026
Narita Nagayasu 1508-1571

@24027
Yamanouchi-Uesugi vassal. After the clan fell he joined Uesugi Kenshin, but angered him with a slight when Kenshin worshipped at Tsurugaoka Hachimangu; thereafter he followed the Hojo.

@24028
Date Sanemoto 1527-1587

@24029
Third son of Tanemune. When his brother Harumune imprisoned their father, his adoption into the Uesugi, Constables of Echigo, was blocked, sparking the Tenbun Rebellion. Afterwards served Harumune.

@24030
Katakura Shigenaga 1585-1659

@24031
Son of Kagetsuna. In the summer campaign at Osaka he slew Goto Matabee and others; Ieyasu called him "the Demon". Known for his good looks: it is said Kobayakawa Hideaki pursued him.

@24032
Nakano Munetoki ?-?

@24033
Date vassal. Harumune's adviser in the Tenbun Rebellion, he became the most powerful man in the clan afterwards. Rebelled under Terumune and lost; after years of wandering he starved to death.

@24034
Endo Motonobu 1532-1585

@24035
Date vassal. After Nakano Munetoki's fall he won Terumune's trust and became Senior Elder. Ran the government and corresponded with Oda Nobunaga and others. After Terumune died, he killed himself at his grave.

@24036
Goto Nobuyasu 1555-1614

@24037
Date vassal; second son of Yunome Shigehiro. Adopted by Goto Nobuie, he took over the Goto. Both wise and brave; for his yellow battle cloak he was feared as "Yellow Goto".

@24038
Harada Munetoki 1565-1593

@24039
Date vassal; a brave warrior given command of troops at 18, he won merit in many battles. Died of illness in Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Korean campaign. Harada Kai of the "Date Disturbance" was his grandson.

@24040
Yashiro Kageyori 1563-1608

@24041
Date vassal. Masamune trusted him deeply and left him the domain's affairs in his absence. Often arrogant, he was later attainted and, after years of wandering, died of illness.

@24042
Shiroishi Munezane 1553-1599

@24043
Date vassal. Followed Terumune and Masamune and won merit in many battles. At Suriagehara he formed the core of the Date army with Date Shigezane. Also served in Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Korean campaign.

@24044
Asahina Yasuyoshi ?-1557

@24045
Imagawa vassal; lord of Kakegawa Castle, Totomi. At Azukizaka in 1548 he aided Taigen Sessai in beating Oda Nobuhide's army; a leading spearhead of the Imagawa's push west.

@24046
Satake Yoshikata ?-?

@24047
Satake kinsman. Living east of Ota Castle, Hitachi, he was called "Lord East" in the clan. Served Yoshiaki and ran clan affairs with fellow kinsmen Yoshisato and Yoshikado.

@24048
Satake Yoshihisa ?-1601

@24049
Second son of Yoshikata. Oversaw the clan's lands toward Mutsu and took part in all government. Befriended Toyotomi Hideyoshi through Ishida Mitsunari; at Sekigahara too he sided with the West.

@24050
Ii Naotaka 1590-1659

@24051
Second son of Naomasa. Succeeded in place of his sickly brother Naotsugu as lord of Hikone, Omi. Fought at Osaka; in the summer campaign he routed the armies of Chosokabe Morichika and Kimura Shigenari.

@24052
Okubo Tadasuke 1537-1613

@24053
Younger brother of Tadayo. Served Tokugawa Ieyasu with his brother and won merit in many battles; even Oda Nobunaga praised his valour. After Sekigahara he held Numazu, Suruga (20,000 koku).

@24054
Satake Yoshinori 1570-?

@24055
Satake kinsman. Living north of Ota Castle, Hitachi, he was called "Lord North". When Sadataka, Yoshishige's third son, was adopted by the Iwaki, he went with him and ran the household.

@24056
Sakai Ietsugu 1564-1618

@24057
Heir of Tadatsugu. Served Tokugawa Ieyasu, who granted him a character of his name. Became lord of Usui Castle, Shimosa, when Ieyasu moved to Kanto. For his deeds at Osaka he was moved up to Takada, Echigo.

@24058
Honda Tadamasa 1575-1631

@24059
Heir of Tadakatsu. Fought at Odawara, Sekigahara and Osaka, and received Himeji, Harima (150,000 koku). His heir Tadatoki married Princess Sen, widow of Toyotomi Hideyori.

@24060
Honda Tadatomo 1582-1615

@24061
Second son of Tadakatsu; a heavy drinker, it seems. In the summer campaign at Osaka, drink made him blunder and he fell. Dying, he is said to have lamented: "Beware of wine."

@24062
Umezu Masakage 1581-1633

@24063
Satake vassal. Moved to Akita when the clan was transferred and served as finance magistrate, elder and more. His diary of early Akita domain rule is a precious historical source.

@24064
Okamoto Zentetsu ?-1583

@24065
Satake vassal; close aide to three heads: Yoshiatsu, Yoshiaki and Yoshishige. Ranked just below the Satake kin, he had great influence and upheld the Satake through diplomacy.

@24066
Okamoto Kenitsu ?-?

@24067
Son of Zentetsu; close aide to Satake Yoshishige and Yoshinobu, with a key role in diplomacy. When Sadataka, Yoshishige's third son, was adopted by the Iwaki, he went with him and helped govern.

@24068
Okamoto Nobutsuna 1583-1649

@24069
Son of Kenitsu. Became a monk named Jotetsu but later returned to lay life. Served Satake Yoshinobu as a close aide; later fought at Osaka and won merit.

@24070
Onuki Yorihisa ?-1603

@24071
Satake vassal; aide to Yoshishige and Yoshinobu with a key role in diplomacy. When fiefs were reallotted, he served with Wada Akitame and Hitomi Fujimichi as Yoshinobu's three magistrates.

@24072
Onozaki Tsugumichi ?-1630

@24073
Hitachi landholder; last head of the Nukada Onozaki, based at Nukada Castle. Fiercely independent, he defied his overlords the Satake many times. Later served Date Masamune and Matsudaira Tadateru.

@24074
Kuruma Tsunatada ?-1602

@24075
Satake vassal. Slandered Wada Akitame to his lord Yoshishige, got him banished and became a powerful aide. After the Satake were moved, he plotted to retake Mito Castle, Hitachi, but failed and was killed.

@24076
Wada Akitame ?-?

@24077
Satake vassal; served three heads, Yoshiaki, Yoshishige and Yoshinobu, with key roles in government and diplomacy. During Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Korean campaign he stayed in Hitachi and ran the domain.

@24078
Edo Tadamichi 1507-1564

@24079
Head of the Edo of Hitachi; lord of Mito Castle. Followed Satake Yoshiatsu, but after his death fought Yoshiaki again and again. Finally beaten, he made peace and submitted.

@24080
Oda Masaharu 1492-1548

@24081
Lord of Oda Castle, Hitachi; father of Ujiharu. Son of Ashikaga Masatomo, the Horigoe kubo, adopted by Oda Shigeharu, whom he succeeded. Waged many campaigns, most of which failed.

@24082
Hori Hideharu 1576-1606

@24083
Heir of Hidemasa. On his father's death he inherited Kitanosho, Echizen (180,000 koku); later moved to Kasugayama, Echigo (450,000). At Sekigahara he quelled revolts stirred by Uesugi Kagekatsu and kept his lands.

@24084
Ashikaga Haruuji 1508-1560

@24085
Koga kubo; son of Takamoto. As an Ashikaga he had deep support among Kanto warriors, and the Hojo took care to win him over. Later defied Hojo Ujiyasu and was confined at Odawara.

@24086
Mori Nagayoshi 1558-1584

@24087
Heir of Yoshinari; Oda vassal. Fought the Nagashima Ikko uprising and the destruction of the Takeda; called "Demon Musashi". After Honnoji he followed Toyotomi Hideyoshi; fell at Komaki-Nagakute.

@24088
Yuki Tomokatsu 1569-1628

@24089
Second son of Utsunomiya Hirotsuna; adopted by Yuki Harutomo. When Hideyasu, Tokugawa Ieyasu's second son, was adopted by the Yuki, he went back to the Utsunomiya; ever after opposed the Tokugawa and Yuki.

@24090
Oyama Takatomo 1508-1574

@24091
Second son of Yuki Masatomo; took over the Oyama. With the help of his brother Yuki Masakatsu he restored the Oyama's power; allied with the Hojo through the Koga Ashikaga and ruled his lands steadily.

@24092
Oyama Hidetsuna ?-?

@24093
Heir of Takatomo; lord of Oyama Castle, Shimotsuke. Caught between two great powers, the Uesugi of Echigo and the Hojo of Odawara, he switched sides again and again to protect the Oyama.

@24094
Tagaya Masahiro ?-?

@24095
Yuki vassal; active in diplomacy, going as envoy to the Satake and Utsunomiya. Arranged the adoption of Hideyasu, Tokugawa Ieyasu's second son, into the Yuki, and was praised by Toyotomi Hideyoshi.

@24096
Tagaya Shigetsune 1558-1618

@24097
Son of Masatsune. Fought for the Toyotomi at Odawara and was confirmed in his lands by Hideyoshi. Later sided with the Toyotomi at Sekigahara, lost his lands and died a wanderer.

@24098
Chiba Kunitane 1557-1585

@24099
27th head of the Chiba; eldest son of Tanetomi, lord of Sakura Castle, Shimosa. His wife was Hojo Ujinao's sister. Killed by a resentful attendant; Naoshige, Ujinao's son, succeeded him.
