@27000
Mimura Motochika ?-1575

@27001
Son of Iechika. When Ukita Naoie had his father assassinated, he took over and strove to restore his house. When Naoie allied with the Mori, he broke with them; attacked by the Mori army, he took his life.

@27002
Maeda Toshinaga 1562-1614

@27003
Heir of Toshiie. After he succeeded, rumours of treason spread, but he averted a punitive campaign by sending his mother Hoshunin as hostage. Sided with the East at Sekigahara and held Kaga (1 million koku).

@27004
Maeda Toshimasa 1578-1633

@27005
Second son of Toshiie; married Gamo Ujisato's daughter and held Noto (210,000 koku). Lost his lands for siding with the West at Sekigahara. Lived retired in Kyoto and stayed neutral in the Osaka campaigns.

@27006
Hachiya Yoritaka 1534-1589

@27007
Lord of Tsuruga Castle, Echizen. Served the Toki, then the Saito; joined Oda Nobunaga when he invaded Mino, and fought the Nagashima and Echizen Ikko uprisings and Araki Murashige.

@27008
Furuta Shigeteru 1544-1615

@27009
Commonly called "Oribe"; vassal of the Oda and Toyotomi. Sided with the East at Sekigahara, but was ordered to kill himself when his ties to Osaka came out. Tea master to many lords; famed as the creator of Oribe ware.

@27010
Kobayakawa Hidekane 1567-1602

@27011
Ninth son of Mori Motonari; adopted by his brother Takakage. Favoured by Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who granted him a character of his name. Aided Takakage in Korea. Sided with the West at Sekigahara; attainted.

@27012
Mori Tadamasa 1570-1634

@27013
Sixth son of Yoshinari; succeeded when his brother Nagayoshi fell at Komaki-Nagakute. In the Sekigahara war he attacked Ueda Castle, Shinano, under Tokugawa Hidetada. Later moved to Tsuyama, Mimasaka (180,000).

@27014
Ikoma Chikamasa 1526-1603

@27015
Toyotomi vassal; held Takamatsu, Sanuki (170,000 koku). After Hideyoshi's death he was one of the Three Middle Elders. Sided with the West at Sekigahara, but escaped attainder as his son Kazumasa was with the East.

@27016
Shibata Katsumasa ?-?

@27017
Second son of Sakuma Moritsugu. For his valour his lord Katsuie granted him the Shibata name. Won fame pacifying the Kaga Ikko uprising; later served Hideyoshi and received the Toyotomi name.

@27018
Sakuma Yasumasa 1555-1627

@27019
First adopted by Hoda Tomomune, he took the Hoda name. Followed Shibata Katsuie and after his death Oda Nobukatsu; then served Hojo Ujimasa and Gamo Ujisato before serving Tokugawa Ieyasu.

@27020
Maeno Nagayasu 1528-1595

@27021
Sworn brother of Hachisuka Masakatsu. Helped build the one-night castle at Sunomata, then served Hashiba Hideyoshi; fought at Komaki-Nagakute and Odawara. Implicated with Toyotomi Hidetsugu; killed himself.

@27022
Onogi Shigetsugu ?-1600

@27023
Toyotomi vassal. Fought at Odawara and elsewhere; held Fukuchiyama, Tanba (40,000 koku). Sided with the West at Sekigahara and took Tanabe Castle, Tango, but when the main army lost, he yielded it and killed himself.

@27024
Kuwayama Motoharu 1563-1620

@27025
Tokugawa vassal; lord of Gose domain, Yamato. Second son of Shigeharu. First served Hideyoshi and fought in both Korean campaigns; then followed Ieyasu and sided with the East at Sekigahara.

@27026
Sengoku Hidehisa 1552-1614

@27027
Toyotomi vassal. For pacifying Awaji he became lord of Sumoto Castle there with 50,000 koku. Later led the vanguard of the Kyushu campaign into Bungo, but was routed by the Shimazu and lost his domain.

@27028
Miyoshi Nagaharu 1553-1577

@27029
Son of Yoshikata; succeeded under the guardianship of Shinohara Nagafusa. Misled by slander, he killed Nagafusa with Hosokawa Saneyuki. Later fought Saneyuki, who had joined Chosokabe Motochika, and fell.

@27030
Tokunaga Nagamasa 1549-1612

@27031
Shibata vassal. At Shizugatake he followed his lord Katsutoyo to Hashiba Hideyoshi's side. Crossed to Korea too and carried the order to withdraw after Hideyoshi's death to the whole army. Sided with the East at Sekigahara.

@27032
Toda Katsushige ?-1600

@27033
Served Niwa Nagahide and Toyotomi Hideyoshi; won merit at Odawara and elsewhere. Fell fighting for the West at Sekigahara; it is said many generals of the East mourned his death.

@27034
Dodo Tsunaie 1548-1609

@27035
Vassal of the Oda and Toyotomi; fought at Yamazaki and in Korea. At Sekigahara he failed to persuade his lord Oda Hidenobu to join the East and became a wanderer. Later served Yamanouchi Kazutoyo.

@27036
Nakamura Kazuuji ?-1600

@27037
Toyotomi vassal; lord of Kishiwada Castle, Izumi, holding back the Negoro and Saika. After Hideyoshi's death one of the Three Middle Elders; moved to Sunpu Castle, Suruga. Died of illness just before Sekigahara.

@27038
Hara Nagayori ?-1600

@27039
Vassal of the Oda and Toyotomi. Slew members of Araki Murashige's family in the campaign against him. Fought well for Shibata Katsuie at Shizugatake; later sided with the West at Sekigahara and killed himself.

@27040
Hiratsuka Tamehiro ?-1600

@27041
Toyotomi vassal; fought at Odawara and elsewhere. At Sekigahara he led the Otani army for the sick Otani Yoshitsugu, watching Kobayakawa Hideaki; beaten by the turncoat Kobayakawa army, he fell.

@27042
Horio Yoshiharu 1544-1611

@27043
Toyotomi vassal. Warm-hearted, he was called "Mosuke the Buddha". After Hideyoshi's death one of the Three Middle Elders. Sided with the East at Sekigahara and held Matsue, Izumo (240,000 koku).

@27044
Mizoguchi Hidekatsu 1548-1610

@27045
Rose from Niwa vassal to direct Oda vassal; after Honnoji served Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Guarded Nagoya Castle, Hizen, during the Korean campaign. Sided with the East at Sekigahara and kept his lands.

@27046
Tokuyama Hideaki 1545-1606

@27047
Shibata vassal. Fought the Echizen Ikko uprising under Katsuie and became lord of Komatsu Castle, Kaga. After Katsuie died he yielded to Toyotomi Hideyoshi; served Niwa Nagahide, Maeda Toshiie, then Tokugawa Ieyasu.

@27048
Menju Katsuteru ?-1583

@27049
Shibata vassal; head of Katsuie's pages, with 10,000 koku. When the Shibata army broke at Shizugatake, he fought hard to stop the Hashiba pursuit; after letting Katsuie escape, he fell.

@27050
Toki Yoritsugu 1545-1614

@27051
Second son of Yorinari; became heir when his elder brother angered their father. Driven from Mino with his father, he served Matsunaga Hisahide; after Hisahide's death, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, then Tokugawa Ieyasu.

@27052
Ujiie Yukihiro 1546-1615

@27053
Toyotomi vassal; son of Bokuzen Naomoto. Inherited Ogaki Castle, Mino, on his father's death; later moved to Kuwana, Ise. Fought for the West at Sekigahara, but gave up his castle and fled.

@27054
Hineno Hironari ?-1602

@27055
Toyotomi vassal. First served the Saito and fought Oda Nobunaga at Inabayama Castle; then served Azai Nagamasa and Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Designed the practical war helmet known as the "Hineno bowl".

@27056
Takenaka Shigekado 1573-1631

@27057
Son of Hanbee Shigeharu; served Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu. At Sekigahara he guided Kuroda Nagamasa's troops for the East and captured Konishi Yukinaga. Studied under Hayashi Razan; wrote the "Hokyo".

@27058
Endo Yoshitaka 1551-1632

@27059
Lord of Gujohachiman Castle, Mino. Suspected by Toyotomi Hideyoshi at Komaki, his fief was cut. Sided with the East at Sekigahara; after the war Tokugawa Ieyasu gave him back his old lands.

@27060
Toyama Kageto ?-1572

@27061
Lord of Iwamura Castle, Mino; married Oda Nobunaga's aunt. When Akiyama Nobutomo attacked Iwamura on Takeda Shingen's orders, he drove him off with Nobunaga's help.

@27062
Tsumaki Hirotada 1514-1582

@27063
Akechi vassal; a landholder based at Tsumaki Castle, Mino, who served Mitsuhide. After Honnoji he met Toyotomi Hideyoshi's army at Sakamoto, Omi; under attack, he took his life.

@27064
Mizo Shigetomo ?-1582

@27065
Close aide of Akechi Mitsuhide, commonly called Shobee. Mitsuhide's man since before he served Oda Nobunaga, he had his deep trust. After Yamazaki he acted as Mitsuhide's second, then killed himself.

@27066
Kitabatake Tomofusa 1547-1580

@27067
Heir of Tomonori. When Oda Nobukatsu murdered his father, he was handed to Takigawa Kazumasu and confined at Kawachi in Ano district for 3 years. Released at last, he soon died of illness.

@27068
Nagano Tomofuji ?-1576

@27069
Second son of Kitabatake Tomonori; took over the Nagano, a landholding house of Ise. Held Okawachi Castle, Ise, with his father against Oda Nobunaga's invasion but lost. Later murdered by Oda Nobukatsu.

@27070
Kozukuri Tomomasa ?-?

@27071
Second son of Kitabatake Harutomo. Turned against his brother Tomonori, submitted to the Oda and served Nobukatsu, who inherited the Kitabatake. Fought at Komaki-Nagakute against Gamo Ujisato and others.

@27072
Kozukuri Nagamasa ?-1604

@27073
Lord of Kozukuri and Heki Castles, Ise; son of Tomomasa. When Oda Nobunaga invaded Ise, he betrayed the Kitabatake and joined Nobunaga. Later served Oda Nobukatsu, the new head of the Kitabatake.

@27074
Kanbe Tomomori ?-?

@27075
Lord of Kanbe Castle, Ise. Submitted to Oda Nobunaga and adopted Nobutaka, his third son. Confined at Hino Castle, Omi, he was pardoned when Nobutaka set out for Shikoku and left in charge at home.

@27076
Seki Morinobu ?-1593

@27077
Lord of Kameyama Castle, Ise. Beaten by Oda Nobunaga, he became his vassal. After Honnoji he followed Toyotomi Hideyoshi and fought at Komaki-Nagakute; later a lord attached to Gamo Ujisato.

@27078
Seki Kazumasa ?-1625

@27079
Second son of Morinobu. Served Gamo Ujisato and won fame against Kunohe Masazane. At Sekigahara he switched from West to East and held Kurosaka, Hoki (50,000 koku). Later attainted over a family feud.

@27080
Wakebe Mitsuyoshi 1552-1601

@27081
Of the Nagano, a landholding house of Ise. Urged submission when Oda Nobunaga invaded north Ise, and yielded to him with his brother. Later served Toyotomi Hideyoshi and fought for the East at Sekigahara.

@27082
Obama Kagetaka 1540-1597

@27083
Kitabatake vassal of a line serving since the Northern and Southern Courts; led their sea raiders. Later the Takeda invited him to be fleet admiral; after the Takeda fell, he served the Tokugawa.

@27084
Takeda Nobutoyo ?-1582

@27085
Son of Nobushige; succeeded when his father fell at Kawanakajima. With his father's military gift he aided Katsuyori as lord of Komoro Castle, Shinano. Murdered during Oda Nobunaga's invasion of Kai.

@27086
Itagaki Nobukata 1489-1548

@27087
Takeda kinsman and vassal. Served Nobutora and Shingen; routed Uesugi Norimasa's army at Usui Pass, Kozuke. Later fell fighting Murakami Yoshikiyo.

@27088
Amari Torayasu 1498-1548

@27089
Takeda vassal; served Nobutora and Shingen, and backed Shingen when Nobutora was banished. Always in the van, he fell in a fierce battle with Murakami Yoshikiyo's army at Uedahara, Shinano.

@27090
Atobe Katsusuke ?-1582

@27091
Takeda vassal; served Shingen and Katsuyori. Attended Katsuyori with Hara Masatane, but was judged a flatterer. Executed at Suwa when the Takeda fell.

@27092
Oyamada Nobuari 1519-1552

@27093
Takeda vassal; father of Nobushige. As Shingen's adviser he won fame at Shiga Castle, Shinano, at Uedahara and elsewhere. Later died of wounds taken at Toishi Castle.

@27094
Tsuchiya Masatsune 1556-1582

@27095
Takeda vassal; fifth son of Kanemaru Torayoshi. Adopted by Tsuchiya Sadatsuna, he inherited the Tsuchiya house. Loyal to the Takeda to the end; famed for "slaying a thousand one-handed" at Tenmokuzan.

@27096
Hirabayashi Masatsune 1550-1622

@27097
Takeda vassal; became head of his house in 1569. After the Takeda fell he served Uesugi Kagekatsu and fought Oda Nobutada. As keeper of Shirakawa Castle he took over Kasuga Mototada's magistracy after his death.

@27098
Fujita Nobuyoshi 1559-1616

@27099
Takeda vassal. After the Takeda fell he served Uesugi Kagekatsu and won fame taking Sado. Left the Uesugi just before Sekigahara and tried to mediate between them and the Tokugawa, but failed and became a monk.
